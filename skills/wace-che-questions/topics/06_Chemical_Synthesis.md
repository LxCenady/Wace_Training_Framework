# SYN · Chemical synthesis and industrial processes 化学合成与工业过程 (Unit 4)

Bank: `CHE/questions.json` (entries whose tags include SYN). 22 of 150 bank questions touch SYN; SYN carries 8.7% of tagged marks. Tags: `tools/parttags_CHE.txt`. Catalogue: `tools/catalogue_CHE.json` (SYN1–SYN5). Key PDFs: `CHE/papers/<year>_CHE_MarkingKey.pdf`.

## Scope
- Multi-step synthesis routes: reagents, conditions, intermediates and reaction types; completing a route with missing steps.
- Industrial reactions and conditions (for example hydration of ethene, fermentation of glucose to ethanol, the ammonia and contact processes as named in the syllabus).
- Rate versus yield trade-offs: choosing temperature, pressure, catalyst and concentration for a reversible process.
- Green chemistry and comparison of two routes on raw materials, energy, waste, economics and sustainability.
- Soaps and detergents: saponification, cleaning action, hard-water behaviour.
- Biodiesel and transesterification: base or enzyme catalysis, properties and advantages or disadvantages.

## How SCSA asks it
- Complete or design a short route from a named starting material (SYN1; sometimes with ORG5–ORG7 parts).
- Explain the conditions of a named industrial process using rate and yield (SYN2). Its parts carry the highest median marks in SYN (6). SYN4 (soaps) has the most tagged parts (17).
- Compare two methods (a fermentation route with an alkene hydration route, for example) on green criteria (SYN3).
- Soap or biodiesel: write the saponification or transesterification equation and explain cleaning or fuel properties (SYN4, SYN5).

## Method in brief
- **SYN1.** For each step give the reagent, the condition (heat, catalyst, acid or base, solvent), the product and the reaction type. Check the carbon count at the end of the route.
- **SYN2.** Rate: a higher temperature, pressure or concentration, or a catalyst, speeds the reaction up. Yield: an exothermic reversible reaction gives more product at lower temperature; higher pressure favours the side with fewer gas molecules. The chosen condition is a compromise: state it and why. Give both the rate and the yield effect in each explanation.
- **SYN3.** Criteria: raw materials (renewable or finite), energy use (temperature and pressure), waste or by-products (atom economy), safety, cost and sustainability. A good answer states the criterion, which route does better, and why.
- **SYN4.** Saponification: fat or ester + NaOH gives soap (sodium salt of a fatty acid) and glycerol. Soap has an ionic head that dissolves in water and a hydrophobic tail that dissolves grease; grease is lifted into micelles. In hard water, soap forms insoluble salts with Ca²⁺ and Mg²⁺ (scum).
- **SYN5.** Transesterification: triglyceride + methanol (base catalyst) gives three methyl esters (biodiesel) and glycerol. Enzyme catalysis is milder and avoids soap formation (state the advantage if asked).

## Traps (examiners' reports and key notes)
- Rate and yield are separate effects. A catalyst increases rate but does not change the yield at equilibrium. Do not say "a catalyst increases the yield".
- Temperature trade-off: a higher temperature speeds the rate but lowers the yield for an exothermic reaction. Students often state only one side.
- Soap explanations: name the ionic head and the hydrophobic tail, not "it has a long chain" alone. Micelles are a diagram or a short description.
- Green chemistry: each criterion needs a reason. A list of words earns little.
- Reagents and conditions: a condition written as a word ("heat") is weaker than a stated temperature range or catalyst name, if the question asks for conditions.

## Generator notes
- SYN1 routes should be short (three or four steps) and use reactions from ORG5–ORG7 plus one industrial step. Give the starting material and the target; leave one or two intermediates or reagents blank.
- SYN2 items should name the reaction and give its enthalpy sign in the stem (for example "the forward reaction is exothermic"). Ask for both rate and yield in each part.
- Use the QNT pattern for percentage yield or limiting reagent in the same question only if the route is stated fully.
- Keep an industrial question to one process; do not put two processes in one part.
- Verify: each step's atom balance and carbon count; yield statements consistent with the enthalpy sign; saponification and transesterification equations balanced (three esters per triglyceride).
