# ORG · Organic structure, properties and reactions 有机结构、性质与反应 (Unit 4)

Bank: `CHE/questions.json` (entries whose tags include ORG). 49 of 150 bank questions touch ORG; ORG carries 14.7% of tagged marks. Tags: `tools/parttags_CHE.txt`. Catalogue: `tools/catalogue_CHE.json` (ORG1–ORG9). Key PDFs: `CHE/papers/<year>_CHE_MarkingKey.pdf`.

## Scope
- Functional groups and classes: alkenes, alcohols, aldehydes, ketones, carboxylic acids, esters, amines, amides.
- IUPAC naming (up to eight carbons, simple branches, one principal functional group) and drawing structures from names or conditions.
- Structural, positional, chain and geometric (cis–trans) isomers.
- Intermolecular forces (dispersion, dipole–dipole, hydrogen bonding) explaining boiling point and solubility.
- Addition reactions of alkenes (H₂, X₂, HX, H₂O) and the bromine-water test for unsaturation.
- Oxidation of primary and secondary alcohols and aldehydes with acidified dichromate or permanganate; full combustion of alcohols.
- Esterification (condensation) and hydrolysis of esters and amides; reactions of carboxylic acids (with carbonates and bases) and amines (basicity).
- Chemical tests to distinguish compounds; deducing a structure from reactions and data.

## How SCSA asks it
- Name or draw a structure, or draw an isomer of a given formula (ORG2, ORG3). Often a short part of a larger question.
- Explain a trend in boiling point or solubility from a table of data (ORG4, the largest ORG pattern by tagged marks, 21 parts).
- Predict the product of an oxidation or addition, with an observation (ORG5, ORG6), often combined with a redox or observation part (2016 SA Q27 tags ORG7, AB9, ORG6).
- Identify functional groups in a structure and classify a compound (ORG1); deduce a structure from reactions and an analysis (ORG9, one bank part).
- Chemical tests and identification (ORG8, 3 parts).

## Method in brief
- **ORG1.** Name the group from the bond pattern (C=C, C–OH, C=O at chain end for aldehyde, C=O mid-chain for ketone, COOH, COO, NH₂). Give the class.
- **ORG2.** IUPAC: longest chain with the principal group, lowest locant for the principal group, then the branches alphabetically. Write every H when asked for a full structure. Condensed structures are fine when the question allows them.
- **ORG3.** Isomers share the molecular formula and differ in connectivity or spatial arrangement. Cis–trans needs a C=C where each carbon has two different groups.
- **ORG4.** Name the forces present in each substance, rank their strength (hydrogen bonding > dipole–dipole > dispersion, with dispersion increasing with molecular size), then say how much energy is needed to separate the molecules (boiling point) or how well the molecule forms bonds with water or the solvent (solubility). Give the property and the force in one sentence.
- **ORG5.** Alkene + Br₂ gives the dibromoalkane; the test is the decolouration of bromine water. For HX and H₂O, give the major product and state the rule used for the position of the new group (the rule the question or the syllabus gives).
- **ORG6.** Primary alcohol with acidified dichromate: aldehyde (distil) or carboxylic acid (reflux); secondary alcohol: ketone; tertiary: no reaction. Colour change for dichromate is orange to green; permanganate is purple to colourless (or brown precipitate). Write the observation as a change, not as a list of names.
- **ORG7.** Esterification: acid + alcohol ⇌ ester + water (reversible; the water is the small molecule). Ester hydrolysis: ester + water (acid catalyst) gives the acid and alcohol. Carboxylic acid + carbonate gives CO₂ (effervescence). Amines are weak bases: they accept H⁺.
- **ORG8.** Give a reagent for each compound, the observation for each, and say whether the observation is a positive or negative result.

## Traps (examiners' reports and the key notes)
- Structures: "Minor error: maximum 1 mark per structure" and "inaccurate connectivity: maximum 1 mark per structure" are the usual keys. Check every bond and the H count of each carbon.
- Keys state that full structural formulae are not penalised. Accept either full or condensed form unless the question names one.
- IMF explanations need the force type and the comparison, not "stronger bonds". "Hydrogen bonds are stronger than dispersion forces" alone earns less than the full chain. Dispersion forces can be the dominant force in a large molecule even though each one is weak (examiners' report); do not assume hydrogen bonding wins.
- Oxidation: an aldehyde is oxidised further to an acid; a tertiary alcohol is not oxidised. Students often miss the second step.
- Esters and amides: the water in esterification is produced, not consumed. Check the direction when the question gives a reaction condition.
- Observations: "colourless gas" needs effervescence; do not write "the product is a carboxylic acid" as an observation.

## Generator notes
- Use molecular formulae of eight carbons or fewer. Give names that follow IUPAC rules; check every locant.
- For IMF, give a table of three to five compounds (boiling point or solubility) and ask for a comparison. Keep the data real-looking but invented, and avoid copying a bank table.
- Reaction sequences (ORG5–ORG7) work well with a small synthesis: one product per step, with a reagent and condition. Check the carbon count and each functional-group change.
- Keep SYN for industrial process questions. Keep PRO for polymers and amino acids (ORG and PRO share 17 bank questions; keep the two pattern sets distinct in the key).
- Verify: molecular formula from the structure; H count; name from structure (rebuild the structure from the name); product of each reaction; isomer count if asked.
