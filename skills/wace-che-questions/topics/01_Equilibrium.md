# EQ · Chemical equilibrium and rates 化学平衡与反应速率 (Unit 3)

Bank: `CHE/questions.json` (SA and ER entries whose tags include EQ). 32 of 150 bank questions touch EQ; EQ carries 11.9% of tagged marks. Tags: `tools/parttags_CHE.txt`. Catalogue: `tools/catalogue_CHE.json` (EQ1–EQ10). Key PDFs: `CHE/papers/<year>_CHE_MarkingKey.pdf`.

## Scope
- Collision theory and the effect of concentration, temperature, pressure, catalysts and surface area on rate.
- Energy profile diagrams (activation energy, ΔH, forward and reverse paths, catalysed path).
- Dynamic equilibrium: equal forward and reverse rates, constant macroscopic properties, open versus closed systems.
- Le Chatelier's principle: predicting shifts for concentration, pressure or volume, temperature and catalyst changes.
- Explaining a shift with collision theory and forward and reverse rates.
- Concentration-time and rate-time graphs for a disturbed equilibrium.
- Equilibrium constant K: expressions (solids and pure liquids omitted), magnitude, effect of temperature.
- Observable changes in equilibrium systems (colour changes such as chromate/dichromate, iron(III) thiocyanate, cobalt chloride complexes).
- Equilibria in real contexts (for example the carbonate system in the ocean, blood buffering).

## How SCSA asks it
- A named system at equilibrium (a cobalt chloride or iron(III) thiocyanate type system, a gas-phase synthesis, a carbonate system). Students predict the shift, the effect on the forward rate and the colour, for several changes in one table (2024 SA Q26 and 2022 SA Q26 are both tagged EQ4+EQ5+EQ9).
- A graph of concentration or rate against time for a disturbance, with the new equilibrium drawn (EQ6).
- An explanation paragraph with collision theory for a shift or a rate change (EQ5, often ER parts).
- K expressions from a balanced equation and the effect of temperature on K (EQ7, EQ8).

## Method in brief
- **EQ4.** For each change: state the shift direction (left, right or no change), then the effect on the forward rate compared with the original (increase, decrease or no change), then the colour. Work the colour from the shift direction, not from memory. A change that does not shift the position (adding solid, catalyst, inert gas at constant volume) still has a rate answer.
- **EQ5.** For a disturbance: (1) which rate changes first and why (collision frequency or fraction with energy at least Ea), (2) the forward and reverse rates are unequal, (3) the rates converge again at the new equilibrium, (4) the concentrations change in the direction of the shift. Each is a separate mark in a typical key.
- **EQ6.** Sketch the rate or concentration on the correct axis. A concentration added is a sudden step; a shift is a gradual change. Mark the new equilibrium levels with a horizontal segment. The axes need labels and units.
- **EQ7.** K = [products]^coefficients / [reactants]^coefficients; for gases use partial pressures only if the question says so. Leave out solids and pure liquids (for example water in a dilute aqueous system).
- **EQ8.** K changes only with temperature. For an exothermic forward reaction, raising T lowers K. Say "K increases" or "K decreases", not an arrow.
- **EQ2.** Ea is the difference between the reactant level and the peak; ΔH is the difference between the reactant and product levels; the catalysed curve has a lower peak but the same ΔH.

## Traps (from the examiners' reports and the catalogue)
- Explanations must link the change to collision theory and the two rates; "the equilibrium moves to reduce the change" alone earns the Le Chatelier mark only.
- Colour statements must agree with the shift. Keys say the colour change must represent the correct shift direction.
- No arrows in written answers. Write "increases", "decreases", "left" or "right" in words.
- Solids and pure liquids are left out of K. Students often include water.
- Catalysts speed up both directions equally and do not move the equilibrium position.
- Addition of an inert gas at constant volume does not change the partial pressures of the reacting gases, so it does not shift the equilibrium. Keep such a case out of a quick question unless you state the reasoning.
- EQ9 colours are whole-question tags: the mark split is by question, not by part. Check the key before reusing one.

## Generator notes
- Use one named system per question. Give the balanced equation with state symbols and the colour of each species. Pick changes so that answers are not all the same (one shift to the left, one with no change, one with a colour change but no shift).
- Keep K questions to two or three species, whole-number coefficients and a temperature statement (for example "the forward reaction is exothermic").
- Real-context items (EQ10) are short: one equilibrium, one disturbance, one explanation. Do not mix in industrial yield trade-offs (that is SYN2).
- Verify: equation balance; K from the coefficients; shift direction against the stated disturbance; colour against the species in the equation.
