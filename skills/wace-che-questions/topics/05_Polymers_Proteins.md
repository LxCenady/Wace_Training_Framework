# PRO · Polymers, amino acids and proteins 聚合物、氨基酸与蛋白质 (Unit 4)

Bank: `CHE/questions.json` (entries whose tags include PRO). 26 of 150 bank questions touch PRO; PRO carries 9.5% of tagged marks. Tags: `tools/parttags_CHE.txt`. Catalogue: `tools/catalogue_CHE.json` (PRO1–PRO7). Key PDFs: `CHE/papers/<year>_CHE_MarkingKey.pdf`.

## Scope
- Addition polymers: monomer to repeating unit, and repeating unit back to monomer.
- Condensation polymers: polyesters and polyamides; ester and amide linkages; small-molecule by-product (water).
- Polymer structure and properties: cross-linking, chain length, branching, intermolecular forces; thermoplastic versus thermosetting.
- α-amino acids: general structure, zwitterions, behaviour at different pH, classification by side-chain polarity (using a data table).
- Peptide bonds: dipeptides and tripeptides, condensation, amino-acid sequence.
- Protein structure: primary, secondary (α-helix and β-pleat with hydrogen bonds), tertiary (side-chain interactions: disulfide, hydrogen, ionic, dispersion); effect of a mutation or a change in conditions.
- Enzymes as biological catalysts: specificity, optimum conditions, use in industrial synthesis.

## How SCSA asks it
- Draw a repeating unit or a monomer for a named polymer; identify the linkage (for example a polyamide from two named monomers) (PRO1, PRO2; 2016 SA Q29 tags PRO1 with ORG2).
- Compare two amino-acid sequences or two protein structures and explain the difference (PRO6; 25 tagged parts, the largest PRO pattern).
- Draw the zwitterion of an amino acid at a given pH or a dipeptide from two amino acids (PRO4, PRO5; 2016 SA Q36 tags PRO5 and PRO4+PRO5).
- Explain a property of a plastic (PRO3) or an enzyme's role in a process (PRO7; often in a synthesis part with SYN).

## Method in brief
- **PRO1.** Addition polymer: the C=C double bond opens; the repeating unit is the monomer with the double bond converted to single bonds, with two bonds drawn outward (the break). Give at least the number of units requested.
- **PRO2.** Condensation: each new linkage removes a small molecule (water for a simple ester or amide). Draw the linkage explicitly (C(=O)–O or C(=O)–N). Check which end of each monomer reacts (the two functional groups).
- **PRO3.** Cross-linking (strong bonds between chains) makes a thermosetting plastic rigid; long, unbranched chains with strong forces give higher melting points; branching lowers packing and density. Say which feature causes the property.
- **PRO4.** An α-amino acid has the amine on the carbon next to the carboxyl. At low pH it is –NH₃⁺ and –COOH (cation); at high pH –NH₂ and –COO⁻ (anion); at its isoelectric point, the zwitterion (–NH₃⁺ and –COO⁻). Side chains are classed as polar or non-polar, acidic, basic or neutral, using the data given.
- **PRO5.** Peptide bond: C(=O)–NH linking the carboxyl of one amino acid to the amine of the next; water is lost. Read the sequence from the N-terminus (free amine) to the C-terminus (free acid).
- **PRO6.** Primary: the sequence (covalent). Secondary: hydrogen bonds between backbone C=O and N–H (α-helix, β-pleat). Tertiary: side-chain interactions (disulfide bonds, hydrogen bonds, ionic bonds, dispersion forces). A mutation changes one residue; say which interactions it removes or adds, and which level is affected.
- **PRO7.** Enzyme: a biological catalyst with a specific active site; it works best at an optimum temperature and pH; it lowers Ea without being used up. Industrial advantage: mild conditions and high specificity; limit: denaturation at high temperature or extreme pH.

## Traps (examiners' reports and key notes)
- Hydrogen bonds in secondary structure are between backbone groups; side-chain hydrogen bonds belong to tertiary structure. Keep the level in the name.
- Condensation polymers release a small molecule; addition polymers do not. Name the by-product in condensation answers and do not name one for an addition polymer.
- Zwitterion: do not draw a neutral amino acid as the form at high pH. Choose the form for the stated pH.
- Peptide order: writing the sequence from the wrong end is a common error; keys check the direction.
- The "repeating unit" must keep the atoms that are in the monomer, not add or lose any; check the number of carbons and the bonds drawn across the break.
- Structures: "minor error: maximum 1 mark per structure" appears in keys for amino-acid and polymer structures. Check connectivity and H count.

## Generator notes
- Use common amino acids from the syllabus-level set and give a short data table (side chain, polarity) when classification is asked. Keep sequences short (two to four residues) and say which end is the N-terminus.
- Polymer questions: give the monomer structure(s) in text (a labelled list) if a diagram is not possible, and state the linkage type to be drawn.
- Protein structure: describe a mutation in one sentence; ask the student to name the level and the bond type affected.
- Keep ORG (structure naming) and PRO (polymer and peptide structures) in separate parts; the pair is common in the bank (17 questions), so state which functional group is the focus of each part.
- Verify: monomer and polymer atom counts; linkage type; number of units; zwitterion charge at the stated pH; peptide direction; by-product named correctly.
