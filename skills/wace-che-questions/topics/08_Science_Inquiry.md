# INQ · Science inquiry and data analysis 科学探究与数据分析 (Units 3–4, inquiry skills)

Bank: `CHE/questions.json` (entries whose tags include INQ). 21 of 150 bank questions touch INQ; INQ carries 4.9% of tagged marks, the smallest share. Tags: `tools/parttags_CHE.txt`. Catalogue: `tools/catalogue_CHE.json` (INQ1–INQ7). Key PDFs: `CHE/papers/<year>_CHE_MarkingKey.pdf`.

## Scope
- Hypotheses and testable research questions.
- Independent, dependent and controlled variables, and how each is controlled.
- Procedure design: repeatable steps, apparatus, measurements, safety for the chemicals used.
- Graphing: choosing a graph type, labelling axes with quantity and unit, scale, plotting, best-fit line or curve.
- Interpreting data: reading values, describing trends, interpolating and extrapolating, drawing a conclusion.
- Errors, accuracy, precision, reliability and validity; random versus systematic error; sampling size and method; improvements.
- Evaluating conclusions and claims against the evidence and the quality of the data.

## How SCSA asks it
- An experiment described in a paragraph or a table (titration, rate, dissolving, electrolysis). The student identifies variables, a hypothesis or a control, and the safety steps (INQ1–INQ3).
- A graph to draw or a table to plot, then describe the trend and read or extrapolate a value (INQ4, INQ5).
- A claim made by a fictional student about an experiment's results. The student says whether the conclusion is supported and what limits it (INQ6, INQ7). Example locations: 2020 SA Q32 (tags INQ2, INQ6, INQ3, ORG4) and 2018 ER Q36 (INQ6 in four parts; part (c) also carries QNT1, AB9 and QNT7).
- INQ7 appears late in an ER question (2024 ER Q36(c): QNT1, QNT4, INQ7). Those are one-part evaluations with a short justification.

## Method in brief
- **INQ1.** A hypothesis states the relationship: "if X increases, then Y …, because …". A research question names both variables.
- **INQ2.** Independent: the one changed on purpose. Dependent: the one measured. Controlled: all others that could affect the result, with a named way of keeping each one constant (same volume, same temperature bath, same mass of solid).
- **INQ3.** A procedure is a numbered list a reader could repeat: quantities, apparatus, measuring device, number of trials. Safety: name the hazard and the control (gloves, fume cupboard, eye protection, disposal); name the chemical hazard only if the question uses it.
- **INQ4.** Independent variable on the x-axis, dependent on the y-axis; each axis labelled with quantity and unit; scale even and covering the data; points plotted correctly; a line or curve through the trend, not joining every point.
- **INQ5.** Read a value from the graph with the grid lines (state the uncertainty if asked); describe the trend in words with the direction and the shape; extrapolate only when the question allows and say the result is an estimate.
- **INQ6.** Random error varies from trial to trial and is reduced by repeats; systematic error shifts all results the same way (uncalibrated equipment, a consistent reading error). For each error, say the direction of the effect on the result. Improvement must name the change and the error it reduces (more trials, a better instrument, a larger sample).
- **INQ7.** The claim is supported if the data show the relationship; limited if the sample is small or the controls are missing; not supported if the data contradict it. Name the specific evidence and the specific limit. A reliable result is repeatable; an accurate result is close to the true value; a valid result tests what it claims to test.

## Traps (examiners' reports and key notes)
- Observations versus inferences: a gas identity is an inference, not an observation. Describe what is seen (bubbles, effervescence, a colour change, a precipitate).
- Use specific terms: "volume" and "concentration" are different; do not use "amount" for either.
- Do not use arrows to show increase or decrease in written answers.
- Acronyms: write out the full term the first time it appears.
- Graphs: labelled units; the best-fit line is not a join-the-dots line; the scale must start from a sensible origin or be labelled clearly.
- Evaluations: "the result is wrong" earns nothing without the evidence that shows it (the value outside the expected range, the control missing, the sample too small).

## Generator notes
- Give a realistic data set (6–10 points, two significant figures or the same precision throughout) with units in the headers. Include one anomalous point only when asking for a judgement.
- An INQ question is usually one part of a larger ER question: a titration or rate experiment followed by a graph or an evaluation. Keep the chemistry correct; check the data against the stated method.
- INQ7 items: give a claim from a student, the data, and one or two limits to find. Do not make the claim obviously false if the answer should be "partly supported".
- Verify: each graph point against the table; trend direction against the data; each error's direction against the method; variable roles against the hypothesis.
