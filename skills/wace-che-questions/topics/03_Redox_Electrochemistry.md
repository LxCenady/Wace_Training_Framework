# RX · Oxidation, reduction and electrochemistry 氧化还原与电化学 (Unit 3)

Bank: `CHE/questions.json` (entries whose tags include RX). 33 of 150 bank questions touch RX; RX carries 13.7% of tagged marks. Tags: `tools/parttags_CHE.txt`. Catalogue: `tools/catalogue_CHE.json` (RX1–RX8). Key PDFs: `CHE/papers/<year>_CHE_MarkingKey.pdf`.

## Scope
- Oxidation numbers, oxidation and reduction, oxidising and reducing agents.
- Half-equations and balanced redox equations (in acid unless the question gives another condition).
- Redox reactions with observations: displacement, halogen reactions, combustion (complete and incomplete).
- Standard electrode potentials: spontaneity, strongest oxidant and reductant, standard cell EMF, why measured voltage differs from the calculated value.
- Galvanic cells: electrodes, electron and ion flow, salt bridge, electrode reactions, cell notation.
- Electrolytic cells: external power source, electrode reactions, electrolytic refining and electroplating.
- Commercial cells (primary, secondary) and fuel cells: reactions, advantages and disadvantages.
- Corrosion of iron and its prevention (barrier, sacrificial anode, impressed current).

## How SCSA asks it
- Compare a galvanic and an electrolytic cell (characteristics, electrode labels, electron flow) (2016 SA Q26).
- Write the balanced equation for a named oxidation with an organic reagent and its observations (2016 SA Q27(b): ORG6 + RX2).
- Label a diagram of a fuel cell or a galvanic cell; explain the function of the electrolyte or salt bridge (2017 ER Q37(e)–(g): RX7, RX5).
- Calculate EMF from the Data booklet table; explain a difference between calculated and measured EMF (2017 ER Q37(h)–(i): RX4).
- Corrosion of iron: explain the electrochemistry and a protection method (RX8, 3 questions).

## Method in brief
- **RX1.** Oxidation number sums to zero for a neutral species and to the charge for an ion. Oxidation is an increase in oxidation number. Name the agent: the oxidising agent is reduced.
- **RX2.** Write the two half-equations; balance atoms other than O and H; balance O with H₂O, then H with H⁺ (in acid), then charge with electrons. Add so the electrons cancel. Check atoms and charge on the overall equation. If the question gives basic conditions, use OH⁻ and H₂O and say so.
- **RX3.** Observations: the colour of the reagents before, the solid or gas formed, the colour of the product solution after. Combustion: complete (CO₂ and H₂O) versus incomplete (CO or soot and H₂O); state the flame change only if the question asks.
- **RX4.** E°cell = E°(reduction, cathode) − E°(reduction, anode), using the data booklet table of standard reduction potentials. A positive E°cell means the reaction is spontaneous. The strongest oxidant is the species with the most positive reduction potential. Explain a measured voltage difference with non-standard conditions, concentration, or internal resistance (as the question allows).
- **RX5.** Anode: oxidation, negative in a galvanic cell. Cathode: reduction, positive. Electrons go through the external wire from anode to cathode. The salt bridge carries ions to keep charge balanced; it does not carry electrons. A cell notation writes anode on the left, then the phase boundaries, then cathode on the right.
- **RX6.** In an electrolytic cell, the anode is positive and the cathode negative. Electrons are forced by the power source. Mark the product at each electrode from the half-equations and the ions present. In refining, the impure metal is the anode and pure metal deposits at the cathode.
- **RX7.** Hydrogen–oxygen fuel cell: H₂ oxidised at the anode, O₂ reduced at the cathode, electrolyte carries ions, water is the product. Advantages over primary and secondary cells: continuous supply of fuel, no recharging, water as the only product (say which of these the question needs).
- **RX8.** Rust forms where iron, oxygen and water meet; the iron is oxidised at one site and oxygen reduced at another. Protection: barrier (paint, grease, plating), sacrificial anode (a more reactive metal oxidised instead), impressed current (external source makes the iron the cathode).

## Traps (examiners' reports and the key notes)
- Equations: balance electrons, not only atoms. Keys award a mark for the correct balanced half-equations and a separate mark for the overall equation; a balanced overall equation with wrong species earns less.
- Observations: "colourless gas" is not an observation unless effervescence or bubbles are described. Do not name the chemicals. Describe solids as "solid forms" or "solid dissolves" with the colour.
- Galvanic and electrolytic: keep the sign and the process straight (anode is negative in a galvanic cell, positive in an electrolytic cell).
- The salt bridge carries ions, not electrons; "the electrons flow through the salt bridge" is a common error.
- Cell voltage is calculated from E° values under standard conditions, which the measured voltage may not match.
- Rust is a mixture of hydrated iron oxides and hydroxides, the iron in it is in the +3 oxidation state, and rust is a solid. The examiners' report uses this as the basis for corrosion answers; write it in those terms.

## Generator notes
- Keep redox equations in an acid medium unless the stem gives another condition. Give one reaction per part.
- For EMF questions, state the half-cells used and let the student read the data booklet. Do not invent potentials; quote the year's booklet values or state them in the stem.
- Cell diagrams: label electrodes, electrolyte, salt bridge and the direction of electron flow in one question at most, to keep the diagram answerable.
- Cross-topic: RX with QNT (titration of an oxidant, such as permanganate) is common; keep the stoichiometry in its own part.
- Verify: half-equation atom and charge balance; electron count in the overall equation; E°cell sign against spontaneity; the anode and cathode labels against the process.
