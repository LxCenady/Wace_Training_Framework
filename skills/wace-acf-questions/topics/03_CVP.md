# CVP · Cost–volume–profit and operating decisions 本量利与经营决策 (Units 3–4)

Catalogue: `tools/catalogue_ACF.json`, patterns CVP1–CVP10 and knowledge atoms CVP.K1–CVP.K20. Methods notes: `ACF/methods_en/03_CVP_Methods.md` (20 past-exam parts, 2020–2025). Syllabus: current 2026–2027 Unit 3–4 CVP analysis (catalogue atom sources cite printed pp.8–10 in the 2026 booklet and pp.7–9 in the 2027 booklet).

## Scope
- Unit contribution, total contribution and contribution margin ratio.
- Sales or activity mix, and allocation of a shared base by that mix.
- Weighted average contribution for a product range.
- Break-even units or revenue, and sales needed for a target profit.
- Margin of safety in units or as a percentage.
- Relevant-cost decisions: special orders, make-or-buy, segment closure. These use avoidable costs, incremental revenue and fixed costs, opportunity cost and unavoidable allocated costs.
- Explaining how changes in price, activity, variable cost or fixed cost move profit and break-even.
- Solving the profit relationship for one unknown (examined: false, see CVP10).

## How SCSA asks it
- One business, one stimulus table, several linked parts. Extended segment questions (CVP8) run to 16–23 marks and carry the closure logic in their final part.
- Special-order and make-or-buy items (CVP6, CVP7) are 9–11 marks each. Both finish with a stated recommendation and a profit difference.
- Short items (CVP1, CVP4, CVP5) are one to three steps, often multiple choice, with distractors built from fixed costs, profit or the wrong unit.
- Explanation items (CVP9) ask for the effect of a change, linked to the stimulus figures and to break-even or profit.

## Pattern 1 · CVP1 Total, unit contribution and contribution ratio
- **Asks:** unit contribution, total contribution, or contribution ratio (contribution ÷ sales × 100).
- **Set-up:** a selling price or total sales revenue, a list of costs that includes fixed costs and profit as distractors, and a unit count.
- **Method:** price per unit (or revenue ÷ units); keep only variable costs; unit contribution = price − variable cost per unit; ratio = total contribution ÷ revenue.
- **Key marks:** each intermediate amount on its own line, then the final figure.
- **Traps:** a fixed cost counted as variable; profit subtracted; the ratio given without working; rounding before the end.
- **Not:** an allocated full-cost amount is not automatically variable (cost classification is CST). A multi-product weighted figure is CVP3.
- **Past:** 2023 SA Q18(c), 2022 MC Q7, 2021 SA Q18(a), 2020 MC Q6.

## Pattern 2 · CVP2 Product or activity mix
- **Asks:** each product's or segment's share of a stated total base, an allocation of a shared cost by that share, or a mix applied to a required volume.
- **Set-up:** several products or regions, with the base given as units, bottles, kilometres or sales. The base is named in the question.
- **Method:** sum the specified base; mix = item ÷ total; allocate = cost × mix; apply a mix to a required total = total × mix. Keep rounded percentages consistent in later steps.
- **Key marks:** the base total, each mix percentage, each allocated amount, and the applied volume.
- **Traps:** the wrong base (kilometres used as dollars, or the reverse); allocating without multiplying; leaving out the total; applying the mix to the wrong total.
- **Not:** overhead-rate application belongs to CST; the mix alone is this pattern, the break-even that follows is CVP4.
- **Past:** 2024 SA Q20(a), 2021 SA Q18(b), 2021 SA Q18(e).

## Pattern 3 · CVP3 Weighted average contribution
- **Asks:** one average contribution for a product range.
- **Set-up:** a contribution per product and a sales mix.
- **Method:** weighted component = contribution × mix for each product; the sum is the weighted average per composite unit.
- **Key marks:** one for each product component, one for the total.
- **Traps:** a simple average instead of a weighted one; mix attached to the wrong product; final figure with no components.
- **Not:** the mix calculation itself is CVP2; using the weighted figure for break-even is CVP4.
- **Past:** 2021 SA Q18(c).

## Pattern 4 · CVP4 Break-even or target-profit sales
- **Asks:** break-even units, break-even revenue, or sales for a target profit; sometimes a definition of break-even in multiple choice.
- **Set-up:** base fixed costs, possibly additional fixed costs introduced in the stem, and a contribution per unit (weighted if a range).
- **Method:** break-even units = total fixed costs ÷ contribution per unit; target-profit units = (fixed costs + target profit) ÷ contribution per unit; revenue = units × selling price.
- **Key marks:** base fixed costs, additional fixed costs, weighted contribution, the answer in the requested unit.
- **Traps:** omitting the additional fixed costs; using unweighted contribution for a mix; giving units when revenue is asked for, or the reverse.
- **Not:** the margin of safety is CVP5; a change in assumptions is CVP9.
- **Past:** 2025 MC Q12, 2021 SA Q18(d), 2020 MC Q15.

## Pattern 5 · CVP5 Margin of safety
- **Asks:** the excess of budgeted or actual sales over break-even, in units or as a percentage, and sometimes what it means.
- **Method:** break-even units = fixed costs ÷ contribution per unit; margin = budgeted sales − break-even; percentage = margin ÷ budgeted sales × 100.
- **Key marks:** the correct margin; for a written part, the idea that it is the sales cushion above break-even.
- **Traps:** giving break-even instead of the margin; subtracting in the wrong direction; describing it without linking to break-even.
- **Not:** the unit contribution from CVP1 is not the margin of safety.
- **Past:** 2025 MC Q2, 2022 SA Q16(c).

## Pattern 6 · CVP6 Special order
- **Asks:** the gain or loss from a one-off order at a non-regular price, and a recommendation.
- **Method:**
  1. Order revenue = special price × quantity.
  2. Relevant variable cost = (unit variable cost + any extra variable cost) × quantity.
  3. Contribution before other items = revenue − relevant variable cost.
  4. Deduct new fixed costs that arise only from the order.
  5. If capacity is limited, deduct the opportunity cost = units displaced × regular contribution per unit.
  6. Gain or loss; accept if the result is a gain, then state the recommendation.
- **Key marks (11 in the key):** special price; quantity; unit variable cost; extra variable cost; total order variable cost; regular price used for opportunity cost; regular variable cost used for it; contribution before fixed and opportunity costs; new fixed costs; opportunity cost; gain; recommendation.
- **Traps:** missing the extra variable cost; regular price used for the order; no opportunity cost when capacity is short; existing fixed costs included although they do not change; no recommendation.
- **Not:** allocated costs that stay are not incremental (CVP.K13); a component sourcing question is CVP7.
- **Past:** 2022 SA Q16(a).

## Pattern 7 · CVP7 Make-or-buy
- **Asks:** whether to make a component or buy it, with the profit difference and a recommendation.
- **Method:**
  1. Make cost = variable cost per unit × units + fixed costs that continue if made.
  2. Buy cost = supplier price × units + fixed costs that continue if bought, after any reduction.
  3. Compare totals; the cheaper option gives more profit; state the difference.
- **Key marks (9):** make variable cost and units; make fixed costs; buy price and units; allocated fixed costs before the reduction; the reduction applied to the buy option; profit difference; recommendation.
- **Traps:** applying the fixed-cost reduction to the make option, or dropping it from the buy option; counting unchanged allocated costs as a saving; comparing per-unit costs only.
- **Not:** a new customer order is CVP6.
- **Past:** 2022 SA Q16(b).

## Pattern 8 · CVP8 Segment profitability and closure
- **Asks:** profit or loss per segment, the total profit, the effect of closing one segment, and a recommendation.
- **Method:**
  1. Segment result = revenue − direct costs (labour, materials) − allocated fixed costs.
  2. Total profit = sum of segment results.
  3. Closure: remove the closed segment's revenue and direct costs.
  4. Recalculate fixed costs that remain: the key treats only the retained share of original fixed costs as continuing.
  5. Effect = new total profit − original total profit.
  6. Recommend and justify with the change in profit or contribution.
- **Key marks (16-mark part):** revenue, labour and materials for each segment. Key marks (9-mark part): retained-segment results, overall profit, remaining revenue, labour and materials after closure, retained share of fixed costs, retained fixed costs, increase in profit. Recommendation (2 marks): justified in profit or contribution terms.
- **Traps:** keeping original fixed costs unchanged after closure; treating the segment's loss as the whole gain; leaving out remaining revenue or direct costs; no justification.
- **Not:** a segment loss alone does not prove that closure helps (CVP.K17). Activity-mix proportions are CVP2.
- **Past:** 2024 SA Q20(a), Q20(b), Q20(c).

## Pattern 9 · CVP9 Explain CVP changes
- **Asks:** how a change in price, volume, variable cost or fixed cost affects profit or break-even, usually in extended form with a stimulus article.
- **Method:**
  1. Identify which figures change and which do not.
  2. Change in profit = change in contribution − change in fixed costs.
  3. Describe cost behaviour: variable cost in total moves with activity, constant per unit; fixed cost constant in total within the relevant range.
  4. Link to break-even: break-even = fixed costs ÷ contribution per unit; quote the new figure from the stimulus.
  5. Conclude on the direction of profit or break-even.
- **Key marks:** for a seven-mark part, how rising variable and fixed costs raise break-even, with the new figure; for a four-mark part, how the activity–cost–profit link affects performance, with a stimulus example. Multiple choice rewards the correct direction after each distractor is checked.
- **Traps:** describing cost behaviour without linking to break-even or profit; ignoring the stimulus; vague wording instead of accounting terms; too little detail (the 2024 report notes this for extended answers).
- **Not:** computing the original break-even is CVP4; a financial-statement ratio effect is RAT10.
- **Past:** 2024 MC Q9, 2023 EA Q21(a), 2020 EA Q21(a).

## Pattern 10 · CVP10 Solve a CVP relationship (examined: false)
- **Status:** current syllabus content with no past question. Do not invent frequencies or mark data for it.
- **Asks:** solve for selling price, variable cost, fixed cost, profit or activity when the rest is given.
- **Method:** profit = (price − variable cost per unit) × units − fixed costs; substitute; rearrange to isolate the unknown (for example price = (profit + fixed costs) ÷ units + variable cost per unit); check by substitution.
- **Not:** a prose explanation is CVP9; a plain break-even or target-profit sales figure is CVP4.

## Method conventions (shared)
- Relevant-cost rule: include only costs and revenues that differ between options. Allocated costs that stay are excluded; avoidable and incremental items are included.
- Write each intermediate amount on its own line; keys award per amount.
- State the recommendation in words and tie it to a profit or contribution figure.
- Use the base the question names for every mix or allocation.
- Carry rounding consistently; round only at the answer unless told otherwise.

## Generator notes
- One business per question. Give a stimulus table with price, variable costs, fixed costs (base and any additional), units and any capacity limit.
- Keep the answer chain short enough to be checked: each part depends only on the previous part's named result.
- For CVP6 and CVP7, include one distractor that is a sunk or unchanged allocated cost, and one that is a fixed-cost reduction that applies to only one option.
- For CVP8, decide up front which fixed costs are retained, and build the retained share into the stimulus so the closure effect is fixed.
- For CVP9, ask for a direction and a reason tied to a figure in the stimulus.
- CVP10 items: numeric unknowns only, a verification step, no extension to other topics.
- Verify each answer by recomputing the profit relationship (CVP.K20) before finalising.
