# GOV · Regulation, audit, ethics and sustainability 监管、审计、伦理与可持续性 (Units 3–4)

Sources: `ACF/methods_en/11_Governance_Methods.md` (patterns, marking notes, examiners' report summaries, 2020–2025) and the GOV entries of `tools/catalogue_ACF.json` (codes, definitions, "not" boundaries, examined flags, marks/awards/years). Syllabus pages for the GOV.K concepts: 2026 syllabus Unit 3 printed pp.10–11 and Unit 4 printed pp.14–15; the 2027 printed pages are one page earlier.

## Scope
- Reporting bodies in the current syllabus: ASIC (regulation and enforcement), ASX (listing rules and market disclosure), AASB (Australian standard setting), IASB (international financial-reporting standards), ISSB (international sustainability-disclosure standards).
- Director duties and shareholder rights under company law and the company's own rules (constitution or replaceable rules).
- Internal audit, internal controls and business responses to audit findings.
- External audit: independence, the opinion on the financial statements, and the reliance placed on it by users and capital markets.
- Ethical issues in financial dealings, linked to affected people and consequences.
- Insolvency (inability to pay debts when due), creditor priority, voluntary administration, receivership and liquidation.
- Climate-related risks and opportunities, emissions scopes, the AASB S2 disclosure pillars, and evaluating sustainability claims (greenwashing, bluewashing). Items GOV10–GOV12 are examined: false (see below).

## How SCSA asks it
- Multiple-choice items that match a function to a body, a duty or right, a dividend or shareholder rule, or the order of creditor payment.
- Short "identify and describe three…" items that name a duty, right or control, then apply it to a named company in the stimulus.
- Extended parts (Section Three) that discuss one or two issues, or explain alternative insolvency actions for a stated company, often in graded rubric levels (general comment, description, explanation, discussion).
- Frequent use of a named company and its directors, shareholders, auditors or creditors. Marks follow the stimulus link as much as the Accounting and Finance content.

### GOV1 · Identify and explain reporting bodies
- Asks: which body regulates, develops, enforces or sets rules for financial reporting; or the role and importance of one body, or two bodies with a company example.
- Key rewards: for multiple choice, the one body whose function matches the stem. For a 3-mark explanation, one explained function of ASIC (for example protecting informed participation, enforcing the law, or making company information public). For a two-body question, the nature and importance of each body (3 marks each at the top level). For the 8-mark discussion of ASIC's influence, the key's lower quality levels are 1–3 marks.
- Traps: AASB (standards) and ASIC (enforcement) are not interchangeable. ASX runs listing and market-disclosure rules, not standard setting. IASB and ISSB are international bodies. Naming a body without explaining its function earns nothing. The Markdown notes say the reports asked for relevant terminology, and many answers did not use it.
- Boundary: the general benefits of standards belong to FRM6. Removed FRC or lobby-group requirements belong to OUT4 (historical only).

### GOV2 · Apply director duties and shareholder rights
- Asks: identify and describe a number of director duties or shareholder rights, applied to the company in the stimulus. Also MC items on dividends and rights.
- Director duties (current syllabus): duty of care and diligence; duty to act honestly and in the company's best interests; duty not to misuse position or information; duty to disclose conflicts of interest; duty to prevent insolvent trading; duty to keep adequate financial records.
- Shareholder rights (current syllabus): attend, discuss and vote in proportion to shareholding; receive audited annual reports; dividends as the directors determine; dispose of shares; ask a court to inspect records; repayment of capital after creditors in a liquidation.
- Key rewards: one award per distinct duty or right that is identified and described. The count in the question sets the number of items. Application to the stimulus is what the reports ask for.
- Traps: listing a duty or right without describing it. Repeating one duty in different words. Stating facts about company law without applying them to the named people. Company formation and constitution purpose belong to FRM2. Recording shares or dividends belongs to EQT.

### GOV3 · Explain internal audit and control responses
- Asks: the purpose of an internal audit; controls that detect or correct errors; issues an audit could reveal; a business or planning response to a finding with an example.
- Controls the syllabus names (GOV.K10): authorisation steps, reconciliations, separation of duties. Each must be named and its operation explained (how it detects or corrects an error or deficiency).
- Issues an audit can reveal: cash or inventory theft, employee collusion, unreconciled balances, unauthorised spending that causes an unusual rise in expenses.
- Key rewards: an explanation of internal audit (reviewing records, workflows and systems to find weaknesses and recommend improvement). One award per identified and described control. For planning, an award for explaining how findings inform operational or business planning, and an award for an example that links a finding to a response.
- Traps: naming a control without its mechanism. No example in the planning part. Spending the marks on the purpose sentence instead of on controls. Cash controls assessed for cash liquidity belong to FIN5. External assurance belongs to GOV4.

### GOV4 · Explain external audit and stakeholder assurance
- Asks: the role of an external auditor; two functions of the external audit for named users; or an MC item on the auditor's opinion.
- Core point: the external auditor is independent of management and gives an opinion on whether the financial statements are true and fair in all material respects. Users include shareholders, investors, lenders and suppliers.
- Key rewards: 4-mark questions have two separate awards, one for reliance by investors, lenders or suppliers, and one for confidence and informed decisions in Australian capital markets. 6-mark questions give a 3-mark-level explanation for each of two functions. A single discussion of the role gives an award for the auditor examining reports and giving assurance to users.
- Traps: naming a function without explaining how independent examination lets users rely on the report. Internal process review belongs to GOV3. Historical appointment mechanics are not assumed to be current syllabus requirements.

### GOV5 · Evaluate ethical financial conduct
- Asks: discuss two ethical issues a named company or its directors have met in financial operations, especially where investors are affected.
- Method: name the conduct; link it to the company's financial operations in the stimulus; say who is affected and what follows; keep each issue in its own paragraph; use Accounting and Finance terms.
- Key rewards: 8 marks for a discussion of two issues linking conduct to company and consequences; or 6 marks for an explanation of each investor-related issue at the 3-mark level.
- Traps: general statements about ethics that never name the company. Issues without a consequence for investors or the company. Calling a numerical variance an ethical issue without an assessed conduct argument. Old CSR disclosure requirements belong to OUT1.

### GOV6 · Explain insolvency and creditor priority
- Asks: describe insolvency to directors; the order in which funds are paid when a company is insolvent; why a company goes into liquidation.
- Key content: insolvency is the company's inability to pay its debts when they fall due (GOV.K15). Priority of claims is set by the scenario (GOV.K16).
- Key rewards: one award for describing insolvency as inability to pay debts when due; for the MC priority question, the one order that matches the key.
- Traps: a poor liquidity ratio alone does not establish insolvency (the catalogue "not" note). Insolvency is about the timing of debts, not poor profit. Do not state a general priority list as fact. The Markdown notes cite the answer option of 2023 MC Q2 (wages and retrenchment pay, then unsecured creditors, then shareholders), but the same notes say the reports give no year for the distractor point, so check the 2023 key before reusing that order. Recovery procedures belong to GOV7–GOV9. A stand-alone ranking belongs here, not in GOV9.

### GOV7 · Explain voluntary administration
- Asks: discuss the process of voluntary administration; or explain alternative actions for a company that cannot meet its debts (9-mark alternatives item in the notes).
- Method: who may appoint an administrator; the administrator's role in investigating the company's difficulties; the purpose for the company and its creditors; the steps in order; the result for the company in the stimulus. For alternatives, contrast with the other actions in one sentence each.
- Key rewards: coverage of who appoints the administrator and how the difficulties are investigated (alternatives question); the process and its purpose for the company and creditors (6-mark question).
- Traps: describing the process without who appoints and how the difficulties are investigated. Winding up belongs to GOV9. Secured-asset recovery belongs to GOV8.

### GOV8 · Explain receivership
- Asks: discuss liquidation and receivership as alternative actions; or explain receivership in an alternatives question where a secured creditor is involved.
- Key content: a receiver is appointed to act for a secured creditor and realises (sells) the secured assets to repay the secured debt (GOV.K18).
- Rubric (8-mark discussion): 1 mark for a general comment, 2 for description, 3 for explanation, 4 for discussion of effect on the company and creditors.
- Traps: a definition without the realisation of secured assets. Treating receivership as an automatic whole-company liquidation (that is GOV9). Stopping before discussion, since the top 4 marks need discussion.

### GOV9 · Explain liquidation
- Asks: liquidation as one alternative action, or discuss liquidation in an 8-mark item.
- Key content: winding up the company; realising assets; creditors' claims assessed before payment; payment following the stated priority (see GOV6).
- Rubric: the same 1/2/3/4 levels as GOV8.
- Traps: a definition only, without creditor assessment and winding-up steps. Using GOV9 for the rescue purpose (that is GOV7). Contrast with receivership and administration should be short and linked to the stimulus.

### GOV10 · Recognise climate risks, opportunities and emissions scopes (examined: false)
- Current syllabus content with no past question, so give no frequency or mark claim.
- Physical climate risks (GOV.K20): climate events or changes that affect operations and assets. Transition risks (GOV.K21): business risks from the move to a lower-emission economy. Climate opportunities (GOV.K22): business opportunities from adapting to or mitigating climate change.
- Scopes: Scope 1 (GOV.K23) direct emissions from sources the entity owns or controls; Scope 2 (GOV.K31) indirect emissions from purchased energy; Scope 3 (GOV.K32) other indirect emissions across the value chain.
- Traps: do not use the superseded generic CSR discussion as evidence of this pattern (OUT1). Do not invent emissions calculations. A generated key for this pattern is not validated by an SCSA key.

### GOV11 · Explain AASB S2 disclosure pillars (examined: false)
- Current syllabus content with no past question.
- Four pillars (GOV.K24–K27): governance (oversight of climate-related risks and opportunities), strategy (strategic implications), risk management (identify, assess and manage the risks), and metrics and targets.
- Method: name the pillar in syllabus wording, then match each part of the disclosure to it and say why.
- Traps: do not infer AASB S2 coverage from the old CSR requirements (OUT1). Do not add emissions calculations.

### GOV12 · Evaluate sustainability reporting claims (examined: false)
- Current syllabus content with no past question.
- Greenwashing (GOV.K29): an environmental claim whose presentation misleads relative to the evidence. Bluewashing (GOV.K30): the same for a social-responsibility claim. Sustainability reporting (GOV.K28): the entity's disclosures in the task.
- Method: identify the claim; ask what evidence supports it; decide whether it is supported; name greenwashing or bluewashing only where the definition fits.
- Traps: the superseded list of difficulties producing generic CSR disclosures is OUT1. Disclosure-pillar identification is GOV11.

## Excluded from current content (historical only)
These items must not be presented as current syllabus content, and no GOV question should draw on them:
- OUT1: historical corporate social-disclosure examples, users, purposes and preparation difficulties.
- OUT2: historical costs and income from socially or environmentally responsible practices.
- OUT3: superseded standalone annual-report or KPI purposes. An actual efficiency ratio compared with a benchmark stays with RAT, and master-budget control stays with BUD.
- OUT4: reporting institutions or lobby groups removed from the current list (for example the FRC or lobby-group requirements).
- OUT5: superseded named financing notes or trust products (examined: false).
- RAT12 is a cancelled exam item and is not used anywhere in GOV.

## Method in brief
1. Read the command word and the count (identify, describe, explain, discuss, evaluate; how many duties, controls, issues or functions).
2. Name each item in the syllabus wording (for example "duty to prevent insolvent trading", "authorisation step").
3. Describe what it does or requires, in one or two sentences.
4. Apply it to the named company, people or users in the stimulus. Use terms such as ASIC, AASB, liquidity, creditors, shareholders, true and fair.
5. Give each distinct point its own paragraph, so each can earn its own award. Do not repeat a point.
6. For discussion parts, finish with the effect on the company, creditors, investors or the market.

## Traps (from the examiners' reports and the catalogue)
- Answers that state facts without relating them to the stimulus. The reports say this was the most common failure across the Section Three patterns.
- Missing Accounting and Finance terms for the body, duty, control or insolvency procedure.
- Ethics and insolvency answers lacking depth and specificity.
- Extended parts run out of time. Attempts fell from part (a) to part (e) in the Section Three reports.
- Multiple-choice distractors: confusing AASB with ASIC, or insolvency with poor profit or poor liquidity.
- Historical content (OUT1–OUT5) leaking into current questions, and the superseded generic CSR framing.

## Generator notes
- Frequency weighting for practice sets (from the catalogue years field, 2020–2025): GOV1 and GOV2 most often, then GOV3, GOV4 and GOV6; GOV5, GOV7, GOV8 and GOV9 less often. GOV10–GOV12 have no past question and must be marked "no past question" in any output. Do not give them a frequency or mark estimate.
- Keep each generated question tied to one named company and one scenario. Keep the insolvency procedures (GOV7–GOV9) in separate questions unless an alternatives item is intended.
- Marking keys for generated items must follow the rubric levels above, with an award for each distinct identified-and-described point. State in the key that it is generated and not an SCSA key.
- Do not reproduce any SCSA paper, key or report text. Paraphrase, and keep any quotation to a short phrase.
- Known inconsistency to resolve before use: the GOV6 priority order rests on a 2023 MC option whose year the notes do not confirm. Check the key before writing a priority question.
- Known overlap: 2024 EA Q22(b) is tagged to both GOV6 and GOV7 in the notes; 2023 EA Q21(d) and 2025 EA Q22(c) are shared by GOV7, GOV8 and GOV9.
