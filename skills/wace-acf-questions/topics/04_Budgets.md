# BUD · Budgets and performance control 预算与业绩控制 (Units 3–4)

Source notes: `ACF/methods_en/04_Budgets_Methods.md`. Catalogue: `tools/catalogue_ACF.json` (BUD1–BUD12). Past papers are 2020–2025 SCSA papers; keys and examiners' reports are copyrighted and only summarised here. Patterns marked `examined: false` have no past question in the evidence pack and are current syllabus content.

## Scope
- The master budget: operating budgets (sales, purchases, expenses, cash), capital expenditure budgets and financial budgets (budgeted income statement, budgeted balance sheet, cash budget).
- Forecast cash flows built from sales and purchases: customer collections, creditor payments, operating cash payments, capital and financing movements, and cash-budget balances.
- The budgeted income statement on the accrual basis, with adjustments for accruals, prepayments, discounts, depreciation and disposals, and tax.
- Performance reports and variances: actual against budget, favourable or unfavourable, and the management response.
- Cash-budget interpretation: shortages, surpluses and the financing or cash-management response.
- Business planning, stakeholder effects, and cost leadership versus differentiation as they apply to a stated budget decision.
- Out of scope here: standard-cost variances (CST), break-even and decision analysis (CVP), accrual statements for external reporting (STM), cash-flow statements (CSH), and the company journal (EQT).

## How SCSA asks it
- A case business with a stimulus table of monthly sales, purchases, debtors, creditors, wages and capital items. The student builds one schedule (collections, creditors, or operating payments) and then feeds it into a cash budget (BUD2–BUD6).
- A one-period budgeted income statement from opening and closing balances, with a list of adjustments in the stem (BUD7). The 25-mark version is the largest single item in the pattern set.
- A table of actual and budget figures with a request to label the variances and give a reason (BUD8). Multiple-choice distractors test direction of cost variances.
- A short "explain two issues" or "describe actions" item on the cash budget (BUD9), usually in the same case as a forecast schedule.
- A planning or strategy item tied to a decision in the stimulus, such as closing stores or an expansion (BUD10, BUD11).
- Calculation items usually carry the working marks, so show the schedule and the carry-forward lines, not just the final figure.

## Method

**BUD1.** Read the command word first. "Identify" needs names only: operating, capital expenditure and financial budgets. "Describe" or "explain" needs a purpose (planning, coordination, control or early warning) and what management does with it, linked to the case. Each point earns one mark. A bare relevant fact may earn part of the mark, but full credit needs the explanation.

**BUD2.** Build one row per sales month, starting early enough to catch collections that fall in the forecast period. Split each month's sales into cash and credit using the credit proportion; cash sales are received in the same month. Spread each month's credit sales across collection months using the stated percentages (lags can be one month or longer). If a prompt-payment discount applies, multiply the amount received by the retention factor (for example 0.985 for a 1.5% discount). Total the collections for each month and check that collections plus closing debtors reconcile to credit sales. The 34-mark catalogue total shows that most marks sit in step-by-step working, so write each lag and each factor.

**BUD3.** List credit purchases by month, including earlier months whose payments fall in the forecast period. Apply the payment terms and track which purchase month each payment comes from. Exclude capital purchases and non-cash items. Each payment identified by origin month and month paid earns a mark, as do the monthly totals.

**BUD4.** Convert each expense to the budget period first. An annual figure is divided by 12 for a month, and multiplied for a longer period. Wages for several staff: annual wage times the number of staff, then divided by the period. Adjust for accrued wages (opening and closing) to get cash paid. Remove depreciation and loss on disposal, which are never cash payments. Apply variable rates to the base the stem names (usually the same month's sales). Interest is principal times annual rate, divided by 12 for each month. Place each payment in the month it is paid.

**BUD5.** Scan the stem for every non-operating cash item. Asset purchases are outflows in the month paid. Asset disposals are inflows equal to sale proceeds, not cost or carrying amount. Owner contributions and loans are inflows in the month received. Shares are recorded at net proceeds (gross less issue costs). Drawings are outflows in each month taken. Loan interest is an operating payment (BUD4), not a financing item here.

**BUD6.** Enter the opening cash for the first month with its sign; an overdraft is negative and written in brackets. Closing balance = opening + receipts − payments. Carry the closing balance forward as the next month's opening balance. Check the sign on every balance.

**BUD7.** Work the statement in this order:
1. Accrual sales: cash receipts from debtors, plus discount allowed, plus closing debtors, minus opening debtors gives credit sales. Add cash sales for total sales.
2. Cost of sales = opening inventory + purchases − closing inventory.
3. Gross profit = sales − cost of sales.
4. Other income: interest income (principal × rate × months ÷ 12) and gain on disposal (sale proceeds − carrying amount, where carrying amount = cost − accumulated depreciation).
5. Expenses on the accrual basis: wages paid adjusted for opening and closing accrued wages; insurance for the period's share of the premium only; advertising spread over the campaign months; rent and interest for the period; depreciation.
6. Profit before tax = gross profit + other income − expenses.
7. Income tax at the stated rate on profit before tax. Profit after tax = profit before tax − tax.
Round as the stem asks. Most of the marks are for individual adjustments, so each one should appear as its own line.

**BUD8.** Set actual against budget and compute actual minus budget. Label each variance. For receipts, revenue and profit, actual above budget is favourable. For costs and payments, actual above budget is unfavourable. For each labelled variance, give a cause drawn from the table or the case (for example a fall in cash sales or a rise in credit sales). Then give a management response: investigate, adjust planning or control the cost. Keys award labelling and reasons separately, so do both.

**BUD9.** Scan the closing balances for negative months (shortages) and large positive months (surpluses). For each issue, give the month and amount, then the business effect (unable to pay creditors or wages, overdraft cost, idle cash). Link the cause to the case, for example purchases high relative to sales. For a shortfall, describe distinct actions: a short-term overdraft or loan, delaying non-current asset purchases, or negotiating longer creditor terms. Give three distinct actions when three are asked for, not two versions of one.

**BUD10.** Name the decision and the case facts it rests on (profit, sales, cost pressures, expansion plans). For planning importance, link planning to resource allocation, cash management, risk reduction and goals, and to anticipating low-cash periods. For financial reasons, tie each reason to a figure and its effect on profit. For non-financial reasons, name the stakeholder (customers, staff, investors, community) and the consequence. For expansion costs, identify three specific costs from the stimulus.

**BUD11.** Define cost leadership as lower prices made possible by reducing costs or inefficiency. Define differentiation as making the product distinct in a way customers value; it is not a price change. For a recommendation, pick the strategy that suits the case (price-sensitive market with cost control favours cost leadership; distinctive products favour differentiation) and give one case-based reason. For a suggestion, name one concrete strategy that addresses the stated problem.

**BUD12 (examined: false).** Current syllabus content with no past question. Method follows the definitions in BUD8 and the performance-report layout: two columns of actual and budget figures per line; variance = actual − budget; label the direction; comment on the largest variances and the management response. ## Traps
- Accrual and cash mixed in BUD7: cash wages paid used instead of accrual wages; full insurance premium used instead of the period share; discount allowed omitted from credit sales.
- Depreciation or loss on disposal left in cash payments (BUD4) or in the cash budget. Disposal cash is the sale proceeds, not the accounting gain (BUD5).
- Gain on disposal calculated from cost rather than carrying amount (cost − accumulated depreciation).
- Missing or wrong collection lag, especially the longer one, and discount factors not applied (BUD2).
- Periodic costs not pro-rated (annual rent, insurance, electricity, interest divided by 12).
- Variance direction reversed for cost lines (BUD8). A cost overrun is unfavourable.
- Generic planning answers with no case figure (BUD10); the examiners report this as the main cause of lost marks.
- Two versions of one action given where three distinct actions are asked (BUD9).
- Cost leadership confused with market share; differentiation confused with a higher price (BUD11).
- Command words ignored: "identify" needs names only, "explain" and "justify" need a link to the business.
- Shares recorded at gross proceeds rather than net (BUD5).

## Generator notes
- One case business per question, with a stimulus table: monthly sales, cash/credit split, collection percentages, purchases, creditor terms, wages, accrued and prepaid items. Keep the period short enough to work by hand (three to six months for cash budgets).
- For BUD2 to BUD6, build the schedules from the stimulus and then the cash budget. Each schedule line should be a separately awardable step.
- For BUD7, use a fixed set of adjustments (accrued wages, prepaid insurance share, discount, depreciation, disposal gain, interest income, tax) so the marks map to lines. Give the carrying amount and the accumulated depreciation.
- For BUD8 and BUD9, use one table of actual and budget figures and one cash budget with one shortage and one surplus. Ask for a fixed number of issues or actions.
- For BUD10 and BUD11, tie the question to a stated decision and a figure in the stimulus. Do not ask a generic planning question.
- BUD12 items may be written, but mark them as untested in the answer key notes. Do not give a frequency for them.
- Verify: every balance carried forward; sign and brackets on overdrafts; variance direction against the rule; accrual adjustments line by line; total tax on profit before tax.
