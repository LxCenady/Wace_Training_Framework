# RAT · Financial ratios and reporting analysis 财务比率与报告分析 (Units 3–4; 2026–2027 syllabus)

Catalogue: `<ROOT>\tools\catalogue_ACF.json` (RAT1–RAT12). Method note: `<ROOT>\ACF\methods_en\10_Ratios_Methods.md` (31 past-exam parts, 2020–2025, with keys and examiners' reports). Pattern numbers in the note match the catalogue IDs (note Pattern n = `RAT<n>`).

## Scope
- Calculating liquidity, efficiency, profitability, financial stability and market ratios from balance sheet, income statement and share data.
- Interpreting ratios: trends, benchmarks, debt position, cash constraints and what they mean for a named company.
- Predicting how a transaction changes a ratio (numerator, denominator or both).
- Explaining limitations of financial analysis and of ratio comparisons.
- Excluded from RAT: preparing the statements themselves (STM, CSH), budget schedules (BUD), capital project appraisal (INV), legal insolvency (GOV), and historical KPI or reporting-body content (OUT).

## How SCSA asks it
- Short-answer and multiple-choice parts that ask for one ratio, often with a table of balances to fill in (RAT1–RAT5, RAT10).
- Extended-answer parts that ask for two or three ratios with working, then a comment on what the change means for the company (RAT6–RAT9).
- Extended-answer limitation parts ("explain two limitations") that stand alone or follow a ratio calculation (RAT11).
- Interpretation parts usually depend on a stimulus: a company's figures, a trend across years, or a benchmark. Answers that do not refer to the stimulus lose marks.
- Market ratios (RAT5) use share capital notes with shares issued at different dates; the weighted average is part of the calculation.

## Pattern details

### RAT1 · Liquidity ratios
**Asks**: current ratio or quick asset ratio; identify what a ratio expresses (multiple choice); fill in a table of ratios.
**Method**: current ratio = current assets ÷ current liabilities. Quick asset ratio = (current assets − inventory − prepayments) ÷ current liabilities. Use current balances only, not total assets or total liabilities. Present in the requested form (x:1 or %).
**Marks**: current assets and current liabilities used correctly; inventory and prepayments removed for the quick ratio; correct decimal places and form; the matching meaning for the multiple-choice option.
**Traps**: leaving inventory or prepayments in the quick ratio; dividing by total liabilities.

### RAT2 · Efficiency ratios
**Asks**: debtor collection period, inventory turnover, or identification of what these measure for a credit-selling business.
**Method**: average receivables = (opening + closing) ÷ 2; collection period = average debtors ÷ net credit sales × 365. Average inventory = (opening + closing) ÷ 2; inventory turnover = cost of sales ÷ average inventory (times per year).
**Marks**: opening and closing balances used and averaged; the right sales or cost base; the ×365; for discussion parts, what each ratio measures and how it judges management.
**Traps**: single-date balances; using sales instead of cost of sales for turnover; forgetting ×365; describing a ratio without linking it to management.

### RAT3 · Profitability ratios
**Asks**: times interest earned, return on assets, or which ratio uses profit (multiple choice).
**Method**: times interest earned = operating profit ÷ interest expense (a multiple). Return on assets = (operating profit + interest expense) ÷ average total assets, as a percentage.
**Marks**: correct numerator and denominator; interest added back in return on assets; average of opening and closing total assets.
**Traps**: leaving interest out of the return numerator; using closing assets alone.

### RAT4 · Financial stability ratios
**Asks**: debt-to-equity leverage ratio; the relationship between borrowings and equity (multiple choice).
**Method**: debt-to-equity = total liabilities ÷ equity. If equity is not given, equity = total assets − total liabilities at the same date. Use the closing balance at the reporting date.
**Marks**: total liabilities as the numerator; closing equity as the denominator; result to the required precision and form.
**Traps**: total assets as the denominator; opening equity; mixing up leverage with liquidity.

### RAT5 · Market ratios
**Asks**: earnings per share, price/earnings ratio, dividend yield, often for two years.
**Method**: weighted average shares = Σ (shares issued or held × months ÷ 12) across the periods of the year. EPS = profit after tax ÷ weighted average shares, in cents if the market price is quoted in cents. P/E = market price ÷ EPS (same units). Dividend yield = dividend per share ÷ market price per share (same units).
**Marks**: weighted average calculated with the months as weights; correct profit for each year; price and dividend for the same year; EPS and price in consistent units; result stated in the required form.
**Traps**: unweighted share count; mixing dollars and cents; using the wrong year's price or dividend.

### RAT6 · Interpreting liquidity and cash constraints
**Asks**: comment on the trend in the current and quick asset ratios; what the figures say about meeting debts; a cash-constraint explanation from a cash-position table.
**Method**: say what each ratio measures; quote the change with the figures; explain that a falling ratio means less cover of short-term debts; link to the company's ability to pay its debts using the stimulus; use "current assets", "current liabilities" and "liquidity" correctly.
**Marks**: meaning of each ratio; trend with figures; conclusion tied to the company. Analysis, not a legal insolvency finding.
**Traps**: describing ratios without trend; generic answers that could fit any company.

### RAT7 · Interpreting efficiency and recommending action
**Asks**: comment on inventory and debtor management, compare with a benchmark or prior year, propose an improvement, or discuss efficiency ratios as performance indicators.
**Method**: compare with prior year or benchmark; explain the direction (longer collection = cash tied up; lower turnover = stock held longer); state the implication (cash flow, obsolescence risk, tighter credit control); propose one evidence-linked action per area. For discussion parts, explain how directors would set a target and respond when the ratio moves off it.
**Marks**: comparison with the benchmark; explanation of each implication; one action per area linked to the evidence; KPI discussion marks by depth.
**Traps**: numbers quoted without comparison; generic actions unlinked to the stimulus; naming a KPI without explaining directors' use. Note: comparing an actual ratio with a benchmark is RAT7; a stand-alone question on historical KPI purposes is OUT3 and is not generated as current content.

### RAT8 · Interpreting financial stability and financing risk
**Asks**: what the debt-to-equity or interest-coverage evidence shows about financing risk; whether the company can support a decision.
**Method**: quote the debt position and its ratio figures; explain the effect of rising debt (higher interest, less room to borrow, larger loss if profit falls); link the risk to the decision in the question.
**Marks**: stated debt position with figures; explained effect; link to the company's financing risk, banded by depth.
**Traps**: quoting the ratio without explaining its effect; general debt theory not tied to the company. A bare calculation is RAT4.

### RAT9 · Investor appeal and profitability
**Asks**: whether the company is more or less attractive to investors, using ratios calculated earlier.
**Method**: take the EPS, P/E or dividend yield results from the earlier part; compare across years; judge investor appeal from the combined direction; give a reasoned conclusion and one limitation.
**Marks**: banded by quality of judgement, not added per point. Conclusion must agree with the ratios.
**Traps**: listing ratios without a conclusion; a conclusion that contradicts the numbers. A single ratio is not a full investment recommendation; project appraisal is INV.

### RAT10 · Transaction effects on ratios
**Asks**: which ratio rises or falls after recording a transaction, "all else remaining the same".
**Method**: identify the balance sheet and income statement effect; trace it into numerator and denominator; a rise in the denominator lowers the ratio, a rise in the numerator raises it; if both change, work out the net effect. Lower profit lowers EPS and returns.
**Marks**: the one option whose direction matches the trace.
**Traps**: tracing one side only; forgetting that profit flows into equity and EPS. Routine ledger entries belong to STM or EQT.

### RAT11 · Limitations of financial analysis
**Asks**: explain two or three limitations of financial statements or ratio analysis for a decision or stakeholder.
**Method**: name the limitation (historical figures; accounting policy choices; single-year snapshot; no non-financial information; comparability between companies or periods); explain why it matters for this company or decision; link to the stakeholder; give each limitation its own paragraph.
**Marks**: one fully explained limitation per mark band; each linked to the stakeholder and decision.
**Traps**: naming without explanation; repeating one limitation in two forms; general limitations unrelated to the stimulus. Stating an adverse ratio is interpretation (RAT6–RAT9), not a limitation.

### RAT12 · Cancelled item (never generated)
RAT12 records a 2021 item that was cancelled because its date was missing, which stopped an EPS calculation. It is kept as the official all-answer record only. It carries no assessable method and must not be used to generate questions, worked examples or diagnostic statistics. Do not use it as an EPS example; use the RAT5 method instead.

## Traps (cross-pattern, from examiners' reports and keys)
- **Stimulus not used**: interpretation answers that quote a ratio without linking it to the company in the stimulus lose most marks.
- **Judgement missing**: a number with no benchmark, prior year or consequence earns little. Compare, then explain what the change means.
- **Averages and denominators**: single-date balances, total assets where closing equity is wanted, total liabilities where current liabilities is wanted.
- **Market units**: unweighted share counts; dollars mixed with cents; wrong year.
- **Terminology**: use course terms exactly (current assets, debtors, net credit sales, weighted average shares, leverage).
- **Time management**: extended questions lose marks in later parts; plan time across all parts.

## Generator notes
- Build each question from one or two patterns and one stimulus table. Interpretation questions (RAT6–RAT9, RAT11) need a stimulus with at least two years of figures and a benchmark or prior-year comparison.
- Choose balances so each average, weighted share count and ratio is exact or rounds cleanly. Give the required precision in the question.
- Market-ratio questions must include at least one share issue during the year with a stated date, so the weighted average is needed.
- Keep limitation questions tied to a named decision or stakeholder.
- Do not present RAT12 or OUT content as current syllabus content. Use OUT3 only to avoid overlap with RAT7; do not generate OUT-based questions here.
- Patterns RAT1–RAT11 are examined (have past questions). RAT12 is cancelled and is excluded. No pattern in this topic is examined: false apart from RAT12, so no frequency is invented for any current pattern.

## Verify
- Every ratio recomputed with exact fractions at the stated precision; averages from opening and closing balances; weighted shares from months ÷ 12.
- Units consistent (cents with cents, dollars with dollars); ratio form matches the question (x:1, %, times, days).
- Each interpretation answer names the company, gives the figure and the direction, and states the consequence.
- No RAT12 content and no OUT content presented as current.
