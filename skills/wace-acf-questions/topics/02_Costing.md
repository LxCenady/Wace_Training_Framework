# CST · Costing and standard-cost variances 成本计算与标准成本差异 (Unit 3)

Sources: `ACF/methods_en/02_Costing_Methods.md` (35 past-exam parts, 2020–2025, with marking keys and examiners' reports) and the CST rows of `tools/catalogue_ACF.json` (codes, definitions, "not" boundaries, examined flags, marks, awards and years). Catalogue status: approved. All 13 CST patterns are `examined: true`, so every pattern below has past questions. The catalogue's CST glossary (CST.K1–CST.K28) defines the terms used in keys and is listed at the end.

## Scope
- Classification of costs: direct and indirect (traceability to a cost object), product and period (manufacturing or not), fixed, variable and mixed behaviour, past and future.
- Absorption-style manufacturing costing: direct materials, direct labour, manufacturing overhead pool, predetermined recovery rate at normal capacity, overhead application, and batch or unit cost.
- Pricing with cost mark-up (mark-up on cost, not margin on sales).
- Standard-cost variances: materials price and usage; labour rate and efficiency.
- Current tasks use the rate and base stated in the question. Historical rate bases and price/variance conventions follow the original question and booklet.

## How SCSA asks it
- A production scenario (a product made in batches or per unit, with materials per unit, labour minutes per unit and an hourly rate, and estimated overheads). Parts run in sequence: material, labour, overhead rate, overhead applied, batch or unit cost, then the mark-up price (2025 SA Q19, 2024 SA Q18, 2023 SA Q18, 2022 SA Q17, 2021 SA Q16, 2020 SA Q18).
- Variance parts in the same scenario or a follow-up one: the price variance, the usage variance, the rate variance and the efficiency variance, each with F or U.
- Multiple-choice items (2020 MC Q3, 2021 MC Q12, 2022 MC Q10, 2023 MC Q5 and Q6, 2025 MC Q5) testing one classification or one variance.
- Extended-answer explanations (2021 EA Q22 and Q23, 2023 EA Q21) that ask for a cost concept or pricing relationship tied to the stimulus material.

## Pattern detail

### CST1 · Classify direct, indirect, product and period costs
- **Asks**: name a cost as direct or indirect (by traceability to a cost object), or as product or period (by function). Also explain these classifications with an example from the stem.
- **Set-up**: a business example (factory electricity, packaging, factory supervisor, office depreciation, sales manager salary) and a choice of labels, or an explain part asking for each classification with an example.
- **Marks**: multiple choice gives the pair of labels only. Explain parts award a classification with an example. Each classification explained with its example is one award.
- **Method**: name the cost object first; test traceability (direct or shared); test function (in inventory as product cost, or expensed in the period as period cost); for MC, both labels must match; for explain parts, write each classification as definition, stem example, one sentence why it fits.
- **Traps**: office costs placed in the product column (distractors of 2022 MC Q10); a classification with no example; generic definitions that ignore the stimulus material. CST.K1–K5 define cost object, direct, indirect, product and period cost.

### CST2 · Explain fixed and variable cost behaviour
- **Asks**: describe how fixed, variable or mixed costs behave as activity changes, usually within an extended scenario, and link the behaviour to an outcome (often break-even).
- **Marks**: one award per correct behaviour statement (variable total moves in proportion to activity, so unit cost is constant; fixed total stays the same within the relevant range, so unit cost falls as activity rises). The break-even link asked in the stem is also awarded.
- **Method**: identify the activity measure (units produced, units sold, hours); state each behaviour on the total and on the unit; apply the stem's figures to show direction; link to the outcome asked.
- **Traps**: describing variable cost as changing over time; describing fixed cost as changing per unit; a direct cost is not automatically variable (the catalogue boundary); the break-even or profit effect belongs to CVP9.

### CST3 · Distinguish past and future costs
- **Asks**: explain past costs (already incurred and recorded) and future costs (expected, used for planning, budgeting and later decisions).
- **Marks**: one three-mark explanation covering both halves. Defining only one half loses the award.
- **Traps**: a relevant-cost comparison between alternatives is CVP6–CVP8, not this pattern.

### CST4 · Calculate direct materials cost
- **Asks**: material cost for a batch or per unit, from material quantity per unit, price per unit of material and the production basis; sometimes includes sundry, packaging, sewing and assembly materials.
- **Marks**: the production quantity used; the material quantity per unit; the price applied per quantity unit; each extra material added; the division giving the per-unit figure if asked.
- **Method**: fix the production basis (units in batch, or annual or monthly units) first; take quantity per unit; multiply by price; add other stated direct materials; divide if a unit cost is asked; round only at the end, to the stem's places; show each line with units.
- **Traps**: batches used where units are needed or the reverse; leaving out packaging or sundry materials; no per-unit division; early rounding; bare final figures with no workings (examiners' main complaint for this family).

### CST5 · Calculate direct labour cost
- **Asks**: labour cost from labour time per unit or batch and an hourly rate, sometimes one rate per department. Sometimes the total labour cost is given and hours must be worked back (hours = cost ÷ rate).
- **Marks**: units or batches the labour applies to; labour hours per unit (or total hours derived); hourly rate applied for each department; minutes converted to hours.
- **Method**: convert minutes to hours (÷ 60) before multiplying; add departmental costs; check hours × rate equals labour cost; divide by output for a unit figure.
- **Traps**: minutes used as hours; the wrong department's rate; batch and unit labour mixed up. CST.K27 covers consistent conversion between minutes, hours and periods.

### CST6 · Determine manufacturing overhead and recovery rate
- **Asks**: total manufacturing overhead, and the predetermined recovery rate = total estimated manufacturing overhead ÷ total allocation base. The current syllabus uses rates at normal capacity.
- **Marks**: each manufacturing overhead item included; the allocation-base total derived (for example, units × hours per unit × months where monthly activity is given); the division giving the rate.
- **Method**: list every manufacturing overhead in the stem (indirect materials, equipment depreciation, factory rent, utilities, factory supervision, cleaning); exclude selling and administrative costs; annualise the base where needed; use the base the question specifies; state the rate with its unit (dollars per hour, dollars per machine hour).
- **Traps**: omitting a stated overhead item; including a non-manufacturing cost; forgetting to annualise; switching base (labour hours and machine hours are not interchangeable); no rate unit. CST.K13–K15 define pool, base and recovery rate.

### CST7 · Apply overhead to a batch or unit
- **Asks**: overhead applied to each batch or unit using the recovery rate and the job's activity.
- **Marks**: activity per unit (derived from total ÷ units where needed); batch activity (units × hours per unit); overhead applied (rate × batch activity); per-unit figure if asked.
- **Method**: keep units consistent: hours in the rate must match hours in the activity; compute batch activity first; divide by units, not batches, when a unit figure is asked.
- **Traps**: applying hours per unit where the batch total is needed; minute conversion errors; dividing by batches; a rate in one unit with an activity in another. CST.K16 defines application.

### CST8 · Calculate batch and unit manufacturing cost
- **Asks**: total manufacturing cost for a batch or one unit, adding materials (CST4), labour (CST5) and overhead (CST7), then dividing by the output.
- **Marks**: each of the three elements per batch; then the batch total divided by the units for the cost per unit. If the stem gives monthly activity, use annual units (for example 12 × 300).
- **Method**: add the three elements on one line; divide by the correct count; round at the end.
- **Traps**: dividing by batches; monthly units where annual units are required; omitting one element; rounding intermediate figures. The catalogue boundary: calculating only one component is CST4–CST7; mark-up is CST9. CST.K17 covers conversion between batch, unit and total.

### CST9 · Use and explain cost mark-up pricing
- **Asks**: a selling price from a unit cost and a mark-up percentage on cost; or an explanation of mark-up with a numerical example; MC may ask for the name of the difference between cost and price.
- **Marks**: correct per-unit cost (not the batch total); mark-up calculated on cost; selling price = cost + mark-up; for explain parts, the concept with an example; for MC, the name (mark-up) only.
- **Method**: mark-up amount = unit cost × %; price = unit cost × (1 + %); round to cents. Example check: $33.52 × 1.30 = $43.58.
- **Traps**: applying the percentage to the selling price (that is margin, not mark-up on cost); using the batch total; a definition with no example; explanations that do not use the stimulus. CST.K28 separates mark-up on cost from margin on sales.

### CST10 · Calculate materials price variance
- **Asks**: the dollar variance of actual against standard material price, applied to actual quantity, with F or U.
- **Marks**: actual price; standard price; actual quantity; final dollar amount with its direction.
- **Method**: price difference = actual − standard; variance = difference × actual quantity; above standard is U, below is F.
- **Traps**: standard quantity used in place of actual quantity (the catalogue's explicit boundary); reversed direction; missing F or U; per-batch and total figures mixed.

### CST11 · Calculate materials usage variance
- **Asks**: the usage variance comparing actual material used with the standard quantity allowed for actual output, at standard price.
- **Marks**: actual quantity used; standard quantity per unit; actual output used to derive the allowance; standard price; dollar amount with F or U.
- **Method**: standard allowed = standard qty per unit × actual units produced (not units sold); variance = (actual − allowed) × standard price; more than allowed is U.
- **Traps**: actual price used in place of standard price; actual output and allowed quantity swapped; reversed direction; missing label. A price difference is CST10, not this pattern.

### CST12 · Calculate labour rate variance
- **Asks**: the rate variance comparing actual and standard hourly rates, applied to actual hours, with F or U.
- **Marks**: actual rate; standard rate; actual hours; dollar amount with direction.
- **Method**: rate difference = actual − standard; variance = difference × actual hours; higher actual rate is U.
- **Traps**: standard hours used instead of actual hours (the time comparison is CST13); a per-unit figure where the total is needed; reversed direction.

### CST13 · Calculate labour efficiency variance
- **Asks**: the efficiency variance comparing actual hours with standard hours allowed for actual output, at standard rate. The stem may give only a standard labour cost, so allowed hours must be derived.
- **Marks**: actual hours; standard hours allowed (or per-unit standard time × output); standard rate; dollar amount with direction. Where allowed hours come from a standard cost, the derivation is awarded.
- **Method**: allowed hours = standard hours per unit × actual output, or = standard labour cost ÷ standard rate if only the cost is given; variance = (actual − allowed) × standard rate; more hours than allowed is U.
- **Traps**: actual hours used as allowed hours; actual rate applied instead of standard rate; reversed direction; not deriving allowed hours when only a standard cost is given. A rate difference is CST12; budget-versus-actual reports are BUD8.

## Method in brief
- **Cost build-up (CST4 → CST8).** Materials, labour and overhead each use the same production basis. Fix the basis (batch or unit, monthly or annual) before any multiplication, then divide at the end.
- **Overhead (CST6 → CST7).** The rate is pool ÷ the stated base; application is rate × the same base for the job. Check units match before multiplying.
- **Variances (CST10–CST13).** Build a small grid with actual and standard for price or rate, quantity or hours, and output. Price and rate variances use actual quantity or hours; usage and efficiency variances use the standard allowed for actual output. Write the direction as F or U beside the dollar figure.
- **Explanations (CST1–CST3, CST9).** Definition, then example from the stem, then one line of why it fits. Do not rely on a bare definition.

## Traps (from the examiners' reports and the catalogue)
- Workings are not shown. The keys award per step (quantity, price, rate, base, division), so a bare final figure loses most marks. This hits CST4–CST8 and CST10–CST13 hardest.
- Wrong unit or base: minutes not converted, monthly not annualised, units confused with batches, the wrong allocation base.
- Variance direction missing or reversed. A higher actual price or rate is U; using more input than allowed is U. Keys award the label separately.
- Explanations state general facts instead of applying them to the stimulus (CST1, CST2, CST9).
- Boundaries: CST2 break-even effects belong to CVP9; CST3 relevant-cost comparisons belong to CVP6–CVP8; CST9 contribution-based profit equations belong to CVP10; CST13 budget reports belong to BUD8.

## Generator notes
- Build one scenario per question with a single production basis. State the basis explicitly (per batch of N units, or per year with monthly output). Keep numbers so the division gives a clean cent or two-decimal answer.
- Give minutes where the key tests conversion, but keep the hours-per-unit figure consistent across CST5, CST7 and CST13.
- For variance parts, give the actual and standard figures for price or rate and quantity or hours, plus actual output. Choose values so that at least one variance is F and one is U.
- For CST13, sometimes give only the standard labour cost and rate, so the solver must derive allowed hours.
- Mark-up parts: use cost per unit from CST8 and a percentage on cost. Do not state margin.
- Do not mix in CVP, BUD or RAT content beyond the boundary the catalogue names. No content from the OUT topic and no content from the cancelled RAT12 item is part of this topic.
- Verify before use: batch and unit arithmetic; hours = minutes ÷ 60 where used; recovery rate uses the stated base; each variance has a direction; mark-up price = unit cost × (1 + %) to the cent.
