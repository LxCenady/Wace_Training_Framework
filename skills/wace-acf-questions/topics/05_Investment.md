# INV · Capital investment appraisal 资本投资评估 (Unit 3)

Sources: `ACF/methods_en/05_Investment_Methods.md` (24 past-exam parts, 2020–2025, with marking keys and examiners' reports) and `tools/catalogue_ACF.json` (INV1–INV6, concept entries INV.K1–INV.K24). The marking keys are the ones cited in the notes (paper and question numbers). All six INV patterns are `examined: true`, so each has past questions; none is current-syllabus-only.

## Scope
- Relevant investment cash flows: acquisition and installation outlays, operating receipts and cash costs, residual value, non-cash depreciation removed, and the after-tax effect (INV.K3–K8, K23–K24).
- Payback period in years and months, using undiscounted cumulative inflows (INV.K9–K12).
- Net present value with single-sum and annuity factors, and payment timing (INV.K13–K16).
- Recommendation: accept, reject, or choose between projects or locations using NPV, payback and the stated constraint (INV.K17–K18).
- Non-financial factors: consumer preferences, competition, regulation and other qualitative considerations (INV.K19–K22).
- The nature of capital investment and the time value of money, including the effect of the discount rate (INV.K1–K2).

## How SCSA asks it
- A single project table: an initial outlay, annual operating flows, a useful life and a residual value. Students compute after-tax annual net cash flows first (INV1), then payback (INV2) or NPV (INV3).
- A two-part question where an instalment is paid later (for example a second payment in a later year). The timing of that payment is tested directly (INV1, INV3).
- A decision question after the workings: state accept or reject, or pick one of two options, and justify from the NPV and payback (INV4).
- An extended question on non-financial factors for the same case, usually "describe" or "discuss" one or two factors (INV5).
- Short explain items on capital investment, or a multiple-choice item on the discount rate or the time value of money (INV6).

### INV1 · Estimate relevant investment cash flows
**Recognise it**: an up-front cost, installation or a later instalment, a useful life, operating costs and receipts, and a tax rate; the question asks for annual net cash flows or the flows that feed INV2 or INV3.

**Method (how the key awards marks)**
1. Depreciable base = cost + installation (capitalised extras) − residual value.
2. Straight-line depreciation over the stated life. It is a working figure only.
3. Annual cash receipts less cash operating costs.
4. Taxable profit = receipts − operating costs − depreciation; apply tax, then add back depreciation so that only cash remains.
5. Outlays at their dates: initial cost at Year 0; a later instalment in the year it is paid.
6. Show a table with outflows negative.

**Key awards**: the depreciable base including installation; the correct life; the operating expenses deducted; the after-tax annual net inflow; the instalment placed in the correct year; the installation cost included in project cost; the residual inflow in its year.

**Traps**: keeping depreciation in the cash flow; leaving installation out of the base; treating a later instalment as a Year 0 cost; omitting the residual inflow; using the wrong life.

### INV2 · Calculate the payback period
**Recognise it**: "payback period" or "how long to recover" the outlay, with annual net cash flows and one initial cost; sometimes a limit to compare with.

**Method**
1. Initial cost as a positive figure.
2. Accumulate inflows year by year until the total reaches or passes the cost.
3. Unrecovered amount at the end of the last full year = cost − accumulated inflow.
4. Fraction = unrecovered ÷ the next year's inflow.
5. Convert to months (× 12) if asked, and round as instructed.
6. State as whole years and months; check it falls between the two cumulative years.

**Key awards**: accumulated inflows per year; the unrecovered amount; the fraction from the next year (not the previous one); the conversion to months; the final statement. Several initial-cost components are each awarded separately.

**Traps**: dividing by the wrong year's inflow; forgetting months; using the total of all inflows rather than the unrecovered amount; mis-rounding months. The MC version (2022 MC Q5) rewards choosing the option that matches the correct calculation, not a near value.

### INV3 · Discount project cash flows and calculate NPV
**Recognise it**: "net present value" with a stated rate and dated flows; sometimes an annuity or single-sum factor is supplied.

**Method**
1. Table of flows by year; Year 0 factor = 1.
2. Even annual flows over a block: use the annuity factor for the whole block, positioned in time correctly.
3. Single flows (residual value, a later payment): single-sum factor for their year.
4. Present values of inflows and outflows as separate totals.
5. NPV = PV(inflows) − PV(outflows); round as instructed (usually nearest dollar).

**Key awards**: correct annual net flow used in an annuity; annuity factor; single-sum factor for a later flow; PV of inflows and PV of outflows as totals; the NPV. Each year's discounted flow is credited separately in some keys.

**Traps**: applying the Year 1 factor to every year; discounting a later payment at the wrong factor; leaving out residual value; subtracting outflows before discounting; using undiscounted totals.

### INV4 · Recommend an investment using appraisal results
**Recognise it**: "state whether" to accept or reject, which option or location to choose, after NPV or payback is calculated; "justify" or "with reference to your calculations" signals a reason is needed.

**Method**
1. Read whether the question asks for accept/reject, a choice between options, or a location.
2. State the decision in one clear sentence.
3. Quote the NPV from your own workings as the first reason (a positive NPV supports proceeding).
4. Quote the payback period as a second reason if asked for two, or where the question asks for both measures.
5. For options: compare the NPVs and paybacks, and name the option the comparison supports.

**Key awards**: the decision; a justification that cites NPV, PV versus cost, payback, or the comparison; a second reason where two are asked. No award for a decision with no link to the calculations.

**Traps**: stating a decision without a reason; citing a figure that is not from the workings; comparing one measure when both are asked; choosing the option with the largest undiscounted receipts; choosing the higher-payback option without explaining why NPV points that way.

### INV5 · Evaluate non-financial investment factors
**Recognise it**: "describe" or "discuss" a non-financial factor affecting the capital investment (regulation, competition, location, consumer preferences), usually in an extended answer.

**Method**
1. Name the factor (from the question or the stimulus).
2. Say what it is in one sentence.
3. Explain how it affects this decision (for example how a regulation changes the cost or timing of an opening).
4. Refer to the case: locations, products, or the figures in the stimulus.
5. Repeat for the second factor.

**Key awards**: the identification of each factor; a description linked to the decision. A general comment with no link to the case earns only 1 mark in an extended question; the rest requires a tied description.

**Traps**: naming without describing the effect; generic comments with no link to the case; repeating one factor under two headings; calling a financial measure (NPV or payback) a non-financial factor. Identifying a legal regulator belongs to GOV1, not here.

### INV6 · Explain capital investment and time value of money
**Recognise it**: "explain" or "describe" the nature of capital investment, or an MC item on the time value of money or the discount rate.

**Method**
1. Capital investment: a long-term commitment of large funds to long-lived assets; give the two examples asked for, not short-term items.
2. Time value: money received later is worth less today, so future flows are discounted.
3. Discount-rate effect: a higher rate lowers NPV; a lower rate raises it. Payback does not use the rate and is unchanged.
4. MC: eliminate options that reverse the direction or confuse the time scale.

**Key awards**: the nature of capital investment (large, long-term, asset-based) and the two examples; for time value, the option that value declines over time; for the rate, the option where NPV moves and payback is unchanged. MC items: one mark for the letter.

**Traps**: describing capital investment as short-term; examples that are not long-lived assets; reversing the rate effect; saying payback changes with the rate.

## Concept glossary (INV.K1–K24, from the catalogue)
- Capital investment, time value of money, initial project outlay, operating inflows and outflows, residual cash flow, non-cash depreciation adjustment, depreciable project cost.
- Cumulative cash recovery, unrecovered investment, fractional payback time, payback rounding.
- Single-sum and annuity discount factors, payment timing, net present value.
- Investment acceptance criteria, comparison of projects, competition, regulation, consumer preferences, other non-financial factors.
- After-tax project cash flows; tax effects in investment cash flows.

## Traps (from the examiners' reports and the catalogue)
- Discounting the wrong year, or leaving a flow out of the present value. Keys award marks per year for the correct factor and for the outlay at time zero; one factor applied to all years loses several marks at once.
- Leaving residual or salvage value out. Keys give a separate mark for identifying it and another for placing it in its receipt year.
- Keeping depreciation in the cash flows, or leaving installation out of the base.
- Payback answers not converted into months, or an answer that does not match the fraction.
- Recommendations stated without a link to the calculations (the INV4 "not" boundary: the largest undiscounted receipt is not automatically the preferred project).
- Non-financial factors named but not explained: give the general comment, then the described effect on the specific decision.
- Mixing the boundaries: initial and residual cash-flow selection is INV1; the discounting is INV3; the recommendation is INV4; non-financial factors are INV5.

## Generator notes
- Use one project per question: one initial cost (with optional installation or a later instalment), a stated life, an annual receipt and cost, a residual value if wanted, a tax rate if wanted, and the rounding rule.
- Keep depreciation as a working step only. Put the instalment in the year it is paid, not in Year 0 unless it is an up-front cost.
- Discount factors: take them from the question's stimulus or tables. The catalogue notes that the original information booklets supply the historical factors; do not invent a factor table from memory. If the question gives a rate and no factors, state the rate and the rounding rule, and have the key show (1 + r)^−n.
- Payback: make the recovery year fall inside the table, so the fraction is less than 1; ask for years and months only when the conversion is needed.
- INV4 items: specify whether the answer is accept/reject, a choice between two options, or a location, and whether one reason or two is required.
- INV5 items: give a case with two or three named non-financial factors (regulation, competition, consumer preferences), so students can link effects to the case.
- Verify: the depreciable base; that depreciation is absent from the annual cash flow; the sign of each outflow; the NPV sum; the payback fraction against the cumulative table; that the recommendation cites a figure from the working.
- Data check before reuse: the notes list 5 papers for INV3 and 4 for INV6, while the catalogue's `years` field gives 6 and 3. Reconcile against the marking keys before quoting a frequency.
