# STM · Financial statements, adjustments and notes 财务报表、期末调整与附注 (Unit 4)

Sources: `ACF/methods_en/08_Statements_Methods.md` (method notes, 13 past-exam parts, 2020–2025) and `tools/catalogue_ACF.json` (STM1–STM9 and the syllabus atoms STM.K1–STM.K27). Catalogue `catalogue_status` is `approved`. All nine STM patterns are `examined: true`, so each has past questions; none is presented here as current-only content. Topics OUT and the cancelled RAT12 item are not used anywhere in this file.

## Scope
- Single-statement preparation of the statement of comprehensive income for a stated period, including unusual periods (for example six months or two months).
- Extract of the statement of financial position: current and non-current classification, net receivables, carrying amounts, intangibles, cash and inventories.
- Calculations that feed the statements: straight-line and reducing-balance depreciation, the doubtful-debts allowance, income tax at 30%, and a land revaluation.
- Notes to the accounts for property, plant and equipment (cost or revalued amount, accumulated depreciation, carrying amount).
- Balance-day classification: prepayments, accrued expenses, accrued income, income in advance, and the expensing of preliminary costs.
- Identifying a company statement and explaining what it tells the user.
- Out of scope here: ratio arguments (RAT), cash-flow statements (CSH), equity movements and share notes (EQT7, EQT8), forecast budgets (BUD7), element recognition arguments (FRM5).

## How SCSA asks it
- One company, one reporting period, a list of items in random order. Parts run from a statement extract to a depreciation or tax calculation, and the later parts reuse the earlier answers (for example the 2022 and 2024 short-answer questions combine depreciation, tax, revaluation and the statement).
- Statement questions are long and mark-dense. The examiners' report names the statement of comprehensive income as one of the hardest parts, so each line item is usually its own award.
- Balance-day and classification items often appear as multiple-choice or short answers.
- Extended-answer parts ask for the role of a regulator and the purpose of named statements (2025 EA).

### STM1 · Prepare profit and comprehensive income (27 marks, 3 years)
- **Asks**: a single statement of comprehensive income for a named company and period, built from sales, returns, discounts, cost of sales, income items and expense items.
- **Method**: head the statement with the company, the title and the exact period. Start with sales; deduct returns and discount allowed; deduct cost of sales to reach gross profit; add each income item; deduct each expense item (including finance costs and doubtful-debt expense); bring in depreciation (STM3), revaluation (STM6) and tax (STM5); end at profit or total comprehensive income.
- **Key awards**: one mark per correctly used item, roughly in the order the question lists them, then the profit line. Each line item is separate, so an omitted item costs a mark.
- **Traps**: leaving out a single income or expense item (discount allowed, dividends, doubtful debts, finance expenses); putting returns or discount on the wrong line; the depreciation, allowance, tax and revaluation results belong to STM3–STM6 and should be calculated there, not re-derived inside STM1. Forecast profit is BUD7.
- **Past questions**: 2024 SA Q19(a), 2022 SA Q20(a), 2020 SA Q19(b).

### STM2 · Prepare and classify the financial position (20 marks, 2 years)
- **Asks**: an extract of the assets (sometimes liabilities or equity) at a date, sorting items into current and non-current, netting allowances and accumulated depreciation.
- **Method**: head the extract with the company, "statement of financial position (extract)" and the date. Sort each item by the question's classification. Receivables: gross debtors less the allowance gives net receivables; add accrued income to receivables. Non-current assets: cost less accumulated depreciation gives carrying amount. Show goodwill and other intangibles as non-current. Subtotal each section.
- **Key awards**: one mark per correctly placed item, such as net receivables after the allowance, cash, inventories, property, plant and equipment, goodwill and a term deposit.
- **Traps**: forgetting the allowance; showing an asset at cost when accumulated depreciation is given; leaving a balance-day item out of the current assets. Element definitions and recognition arguments belong to FRM5; separate asset notes belong to STM8.
- **Past questions**: 2024 SA Q19(c), 2022 SA Q20(b).

### STM3 · Calculate depreciation for the reporting period (16 marks, 3 years)
- **Asks**: depreciation on named assets at a stated rate, for a part-year period, applied to cost (straight-line) or carrying amount (reducing-balance).
- **Method**: identify each asset class, its cost and rate; choose the base for the method; apply the period fraction (for example 6/12 or 2/12); round to the nearest dollar as the key does; the charge becomes an expense in STM1 and adds to opening accumulated depreciation to give the closing figure for the notes or statement.
- **Key awards**: separate marks for the correct cost, the correct rate, the correct period fraction, deducting accumulated depreciation where needed, and carrying the figure through.
- **Traps**: a full-year rate on a part-year period; cost where carrying amount is required; the closing accumulated depreciation left without the opening balance. Showing an already-given accumulated balance is STM8 or STM2 work. Removing non-cash depreciation for a cash-flow statement belongs to CSH or INV1.
- **Past questions**: 2024 SA Q19(a) and Q19(b), 2022 SA Q20(a) and Q20(b), 2021 SA Q17(a), 2020 SA Q19(b).

### STM4 · Calculate doubtful debts through the allowance (3 marks, 1 year)
- **Asks**: the doubtful-debts expense from an opening allowance, bad debts written off, and the required closing allowance.
- **Method**: set up the allowance ledger with the opening balance; post the write-offs as a debit to the allowance; enter the closing balance; solve closing = opening + expense − write-offs, so expense = closing − opening + write-offs; show the expense in STM1.
- **Key awards**: one mark each for recording the write-offs in the allowance, using the opening balance, and using the closing balance.
- **Traps**: leaving out the write-offs or the opening balance; treating write-offs as the whole period expense; deducting the allowance from receivables here (that is STM2 work).
- **Past questions**: 2020 SA Q19(b).

### STM5 · Calculate income tax and after-tax profit (5 marks, 3 years)
- **Asks**: income tax at the stated rate (30% in these papers) on the correct profit before tax, and sometimes profit after tax.
- **Method**: take the profit before tax from the completed statement; tax = PBT × 30%; PAT = PBT − tax, which equals PBT × 70%; carry the result to the statement and any ratio that uses it.
- **Key awards**: marks for the correct PBT, the 30% rate, the tax expense, and the after-tax profit.
- **Traps**: using a PBT figure other than the one in the question; omitting the rate. If the key contains an arithmetic typo in the source, record it rather than copying it. Historical cash tax payments belong to CSH6.
- **Past questions**: 2024 SA Q19(a), 2022 SA Q19(a) and Q19(b), 2022 SA Q20(a), 2021 SA Q17(a), 2020 SA Q19(b).

### STM6 · Calculate and present revaluation gains (5 marks, 3 years)
- **Asks**: the revaluation gain on an asset (usually land) from its specified value and prior carrying amount, and where it is shown.
- **Method**: gain = specified value − carrying amount; show the gain in other comprehensive income below profit in STM1; add the revalued amount to the asset in the statement of financial position (and in the PPE note if asked).
- **Key awards**: marks for using the specified value, for the gain in comprehensive income, and for the asset adjustment. The gain must appear in both places.
- **Traps**: measuring against the wrong base; putting the gain in profit; showing it in only one of the two places. An equity-reserve movement belongs to EQT7; routine asset notes belong to STM8.
- **Past questions**: 2024 SA Q19(a), 2022 SA Q20(a) and Q20(b), 2020 SA Q19(b).

### STM7 · Identify and explain financial statements (4 marks available, 2 awarded, 1 year)
- **Asks**: the role of the regulator in company financial reporting (ASIC in these papers) and which general-purpose statements a named company must prepare, with what each tells the user.
- **Method**: explain the regulator's role in shaping general-purpose reporting by companies; name a statement from the general-purpose set and outline the information it gives the user for the period or date stated; name a second statement and outline it; link both to the company in the question.
- **Key awards**: the catalogue awards 2 of the 4 marks. Identifying a statement without outlining its information earns only the identification mark.
- **Traps**: naming without explaining; a detailed ratio argument is RAT work; a calculation belongs to the preparation pattern.
- **Past questions**: 2025 EA Q22(d).

### STM8 · Prepare property, plant and equipment notes (12 marks, 2 years)
- **Asks**: a note showing, for each asset class, cost or revalued amount, opening and closing accumulated depreciation, the period charge and the carrying amount. Some versions also ask for share, reserve or dividend notes; those belong to EQT8.
- **Method**: head the note; for each class show cost at the start; add any revaluation; show opening accumulated depreciation, the period depreciation from STM3, and closing accumulated depreciation; carrying amount = cost (or revalued amount) − closing accumulated depreciation; total the classes.
- **Key awards**: one mark per correct figure in the note (cost, revaluation, opening accumulated depreciation, period charge, closing accumulated depreciation, carrying amount, each class shown).
- **Traps**: missing the land revaluation; using the period charge as closing accumulated depreciation without the opening balance; omitting a class. New depreciation calculations belong to STM3.
- **Past questions**: 2024 SA Q19(b), 2021 SA Q19(b).

### STM9 · Apply balance-day and expense classifications (2 marks, 2 years)
- **Asks**: classify a balance-day item (prepayment, accrued expense, accrued income, income in advance) or an expense (including preliminary costs), often as a multiple-choice item or a one-line explanation.
- **Method**: ask (1) has cash moved, and (2) has the good or service been provided? Paid, not yet used: prepayment (asset). Used, not yet paid: accrued expense (liability). Earned, not yet received: accrued income (in receivables). Received, not yet earned: income in advance (liability). Preliminary costs are expensed in the period, as the 2024 multiple-choice key shows.
- **Key awards**: one mark for the correct option; for the short-answer form, one mark for accrued income in receivables and one for prepayments in other current assets.
- **Traps**: calling a prepayment a liability; confusing accrued income with income in advance; leaving accrued income out of receivables. A historical cost of a preliminary item is not automatically an asset. Element recognition reasoning is FRM5; forecast adjustments are BUD7.
- **Past questions**: 2024 MC Q15, 2022 SA Q20(b), 2021 MC Q7.

## Traps across the topic (from the notes and the catalogue)
- Omitted line items cost marks in STM1 more than any other error. Tick each figure in the question against the statement.
- Depreciation: check the fraction of the period, the base (cost or carrying amount), and the opening accumulated balance.
- Tax: the base is the PBT in the question; the rate is 30%.
- Revaluation: the gain goes to other comprehensive income and to the asset. Both or neither.
- Balance-day: prepayment is an asset, income in advance is a liability, accrued income sits in receivables.
- Keep patterns apart: a calculation feeds a statement; it is not itself a statement question. Cross-topic boundaries are listed in each pattern above.

## Generator notes
- Build one company, one period (use an unusual length in about one in three questions), 4–6 parts. Each part should reuse an answer from an earlier part (depreciation → statement → tax → notes), as SCSA does.
- Give every figure in the stem, in a list or table, in any order. Include one distractor per question (a balance-day item or a non-current item that must be sorted out).
- Pick a single revaluation or single doubtful-debts reconciliation per paper, not both at once.
- Write marking keys with one row per award: each line item, each rate or fraction, each total, the tax line, the profit line.
- Verify before showing: depreciation = base × rate × period fraction, rounded to the nearest dollar; allowance: closing = opening + expense − write-offs; tax = PBT × 0.30 and PAT = PBT × 0.70; receivables net of allowance; revaluation gain = specified value − carrying amount, and the same figure in both places; if a full statement of financial position is built, assets = liabilities + equity.
- Use Australian companies and everyday contexts (a retailer, a café, a cafe fit-out, a delivery van, a block of land). Do not use real company names or real published figures.
