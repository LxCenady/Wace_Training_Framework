# FIN · Finance products and asset management 金融产品与资产管理 (Unit 3)

Sources: `ACF/methods_en/01_Finance_Methods.md` (14 past-exam parts, 2020–2025, with keys and examiners' reports) and the FIN entries of `tools/catalogue_ACF.json` (FIN1–FIN5, knowledge FIN.K1–FIN.K21). Catalogue status: approved 2026-10-15. All five FIN patterns are `examined: true`, so each has past questions; no FIN pattern is an `examined: false` item. The two `examined: false` entries in the catalogue (OUT5, OUT6) belong to topic OUT and are not used here.

## Scope
- Management versus financial accounting: purpose, users and outputs (FIN1).
- Classifying, describing and selecting financing products by term, security, ownership or purpose (FIN2; FIN.K3–FIN.K11).
- Principles of asset and funding management: non-current assets, receivables, inventory, cash, short- and long-term debt, and appropriate equity capital; undercapitalisation and overcapitalisation (FIN3; FIN.K12–FIN.K21). Receivables, inventory and debt management (FIN.K16–FIN.K18) are covered only through FIN3's definition; no separate pattern exists.
- Why cash matters for viability (FIN4) and cash-management controls (FIN5); FIN.K14 (liquidity) and FIN.K15 (cash-management controls) support both.

## Boundaries (other topics)
- Ratio calculations (gearing, profitability, efficiency) belong to RAT. Asset cash-flow calculations belong to CSH.
- Cash-budget construction and budget control belong to BUD. A cash-budget arithmetic check does not earn FIN4 or FIN5.
- Recording share issues and dividends belongs to EQT. Capital-project appraisal belongs to INV.
- Cash versus accrual comparison is FRM9, not FIN1.
- Reporting-entity requirements belong to FRM. ASIC, AASB, ASX and ISSB roles belong to GOV.

## Historical content (keep out of current items)
- Superseded named products, including the notes and trust products that older past questions used, are topic OUT5 and must not be presented as current. Use the current wording "unsecured borrowing" (FIN.K6) rather than an old product name.
- RAT12 (the cancelled 2021 administrative-credit item) carries no assessable content and is excluded from all generation and statistics.

## How SCSA asks it
- A stem with a short list of named financing options or a business scenario, asking the student to identify, describe or select one source (FIN2), often with three descriptions in one part.
- A multiple-choice item that contrasts internal and external accounting purposes, or classifies a finance source (FIN1, FIN2).
- A "why" discussion about appropriate asset or equity investment, or the consequence of undercapitalisation (FIN3).
- An extended-answer part (2025 Question 22, extended answer) that asks why cash matters to a named business, followed by two cash-management strategies (FIN4, FIN5). The two sub-patterns share one part.

## Pattern FIN1: Compare management and financial accounting

**Operations**: identify, compare, describe.

**Recognise it**: a stem contrasting internal decision-making with external reporting. Typical signals: "focuses primarily on", "day-to-day", "key document for internal reporting", "external users", "legal obligations and compliance".

**Method**
1. Identify the audience: managers (internal) or outside users such as shareholders, creditors and regulators (external).
2. Identify the purpose: planning, coordinating and controlling activity (management accounting) or general-purpose reporting for legal and compliance needs (financial accounting).
3. Identify the output: budgets, forecasts and performance reports point to management accounting; the statement of financial position, statement of comprehensive income and statement of changes in equity point to financial accounting.
4. Choose the option that fits audience and output. Reject any option that matches only one of them.

**Key awards**: one mark for the single correct option in a multiple-choice item. The keys in the evidence are answer-only, so the correct option states the right purpose for the stated audience. In extended form, the key looks for identification, a comparison on purpose and users, and a description of outputs.

**Traps**: treating management accounting as year-end reporting; offering a formal financial statement as an internal decision tool; reversing the audiences. The evidence has no examiner report for this pattern, so these traps come from the stems and keys.

**Generator notes**: keep one clear audience per option. Avoid options that differ only by a word such as "internal" versus "external" without changing the purpose.

## Pattern FIN2: Classify and select financing products

**Operations**: identify, describe, justify-choice.

**Recognise it**: a request to identify, describe or select a source of finance. Signals: "short term or long term", "money market", "term loan", "bank overdraft", "supplier credit", "ordinary shares", "debentures", "unsecured borrowing", "no security over the loan", "describe three types of long-term business finance".

**Method**
1. Classify by term: short-term (money-market products, overdraft, supplier credit) versus long-term (term loan, shares, debentures).
2. Classify by security: secured borrowing (lender holds a claim over specified assets) versus unsecured borrowing.
3. Classify by ownership: equity financing (ordinary shares) versus debt financing (loans, debentures).
4. Match the source to the need. Non-current asset purchases are normally financed long-term; a short-term surplus or working-capital gap points to short-term sources. Term deposits are classified by their maturity (FIN.K11).
5. For a description, give for each source: what it is, its term, its security or ownership, and one reason it suits the stated business. One suitable, fully described source earns one award.

**Key awards**: for multiple-choice items, the one option matching the stated term, security or purpose. For 2022 EA Q22(c), one mark per suitable long-term source that is both identified and described, up to three. Naming alone earns nothing.

**Traps**: calling a money-market investment long-term; choosing ordinary shares as a short-term source; listing names without description; confusing secured and unsecured borrowing; using an old product name (see Historical content).

**Generator notes**: use current product names from FIN.K3–FIN.K11 (money-market products, cash management accounts, term deposits, debentures, term loans, overdrafts, supplier credit, ordinary shares). Describe security in words such as "secured over specified assets". Do not use "notes" or "trusts" as current products.

## Pattern FIN3: Apply principles of asset and funding management

**Operations**: identify, explain.

**Recognise it**: a stem about balancing assets and funding. Signals: "appropriate level of equity capital", "undercapitalised", "overcapitalised", "insufficient equity", "investment in non-current assets", "maintaining appropriate levels of investment".

**Method**
1. Name the principle being tested: appropriate investment in non-current assets, or an appropriate level of equity capital.
2. Work out the consequence. Undercapitalisation (FIN.K20) can leave the business without funds to renew non-current assets when they are needed, and can leave working capital short, raising the risk of business failure. Overcapitalisation (FIN.K21) means capital in excess of the resource requirement.
3. For discussion, explain why appropriate asset investment supports growth, revenue, financing or operating capacity, and what happens when the level is too low.
4. Treat the balance between assets and funding as the principle. Do not assume that more investment or more of one funding source is always better.

**Key awards**: for multiple-choice items, the one consequence that matches the principle. For 2025 SA Q21(d) (4 marks), the key awards a discussion of why appropriate non-current-asset investment supports growth, revenue, financing or operating capacity. The evidence records only one award for this part, so check the full key before relying on any split.

**Traps**: treating continual growth of non-current assets as a sound principle; confusing undercapitalised with overcapitalised; treating less equity as a route to higher profit; calculating gearing or profitability ratios here (those are RAT).

**Generator notes**: the answer is a principle and its consequence, not a number. Avoid stems that need a ratio.

## Pattern FIN4: Explain cash requirements for business viability

**Operations**: explain, evaluate.

**Recognise it**: an extended-answer part asking why cash matters to a named business's viability. In 2025 EA Q22(a) it is followed by a request for two cash-management strategies (FIN5).

**Method**
1. Open with the direct link: cash meets debts, wages and suppliers as they fall due, so a business can fail for lack of cash even when profitable.
2. Explain how cash supports ongoing operations and the development of the named business (FIN.K14 liquidity).
3. Build to discussion: state the point, explain why it matters for viability, weigh the consequence of a shortage or surplus, and conclude.
4. Move to the strategies (FIN5) only after the importance discussion.

**Key awards**: the rubric for the importance discussion gives 1 mark for a general comment, 2 for description, 3 for explanation and 4 for discussion. A general statement about cash earns the lowest level only.

**Traps**: stopping at a general comment; discussing cash without linking to the named business; treating a cash-budget arithmetic result as the answer (BUD).

**Marks note**: the notes describe the 2025 EA Q22(a) as an 8-mark part covering this pattern and FIN5. The catalogue records FIN4 as 4 marks and FIN5 as 4 marks, which together make 8. The split is not confirmed by the key in the evidence, so check the full key before setting a mark allocation.

**Generator notes**: use one named business with a clear cash situation. The question should ask for an explanation and discussion, not a calculation.

## Pattern FIN5: Recommend cash-management controls

**Operations**: describe, justify-choice.

**Recognise it**: the second half of an extended-answer part asking for two strategies to manage a business's cash. In 2025 EA Q22(a) it follows the importance discussion.

**Method**
1. Choose a practical cash-management action that fits the business in the stem. The key accepts any relevant strategy.
2. Describe how it works: what the business does, how often, and who is responsible (for example, a regular cash-flow forecast reviewed by a named manager, or a set schedule for invoicing and collecting receivables).
3. Connect it to the objective: keep enough cash to meet obligations as they fall due, without holding idle cash (FIN.K15, FIN.K16).
4. Write two separate strategies, each fully described.

**Key awards**: one award for each relevant strategy that is described. A relevant strategy that is only identified earns 1 mark. A strategy that does not relate to the cash position earns nothing.

**Traps**: naming a strategy without describing it; strategies unrelated to cash (for example, a product-pricing change that does not touch cash timing); an arithmetic cash balance as the strategy (BUD).

**Generator notes**: ask for two strategies, not three, so the mark split is clear. Give the business a cash problem the strategies can address.

## Method summary and checks
- Match each stem to one pattern first. FIN4 and FIN5 share one part, so check the mark split in the key before reuse.
- Every source or strategy answer needs description. Naming alone is marked down in FIN2 and FIN5.
- Check that the answer does not drift into RAT, CSH, BUD or EQT content.
- Verify that all product names are current (FIN.K3–FIN.K11), and that no superseded name appears.

## Generator notes
- Use one named business or one scenario per item. Give the business enough detail (type, size, asset needs, cash position) for the answer to be specific.
- Mix the recognition styles: a multiple-choice classification (FIN1 or FIN2), a description with three sources (FIN2), a principle discussion (FIN3), and an extended two-part item (FIN4 and FIN5).
- Write the key with marks per distinct award, using the award counts above as a guide for the total.
- Verify: the term, security and ownership of each product; the consequence named in each FIN3 answer; the audience and output in each FIN1 option; and that no OUT or RAT12 content appears.
