# CSH · Statements of cash flows 现金流量表 (Units 3–4; current syllabus 2026–2027)

Sources: `ACF/methods_en/09_Cash_Flow_Methods.md` (14 past-exam parts, 2020–2025 papers, marking keys and examiners' reports) and the CSH entries in `tools/catalogue_ACF.json` (CSH1–CSH12, knowledge atoms CSH.K1–CSH.K20). All twelve CSH patterns are `examined: true`, so each has past-question evidence. None is `examined: false`.

## Scope
- Definitions of cash and cash equivalents, including the liquidity, maturity and risk conditions (CSH.K1, CSH.K2, CSH.K20).
- Reconciliations that turn accrual balances into cash receipts and payments: customers, suppliers, other operating expenses, interest, income tax, asset and investment purchases, asset disposals, financing, and the opening and closing cash totals (CSH.K3–CSH.K14).
- Classification of cash flows into operating, investing and financing activities (CSH.K15–CSH.K17).
- Profit versus cash flow, and the benefits of cash-flow information to users (CSH.K18, CSH.K19; CSH11).
- Interpretation of a prepared statement and the business implications of its totals (CSH12).

Boundaries (from the catalogue `not` fields):
- Forecast cash receipts and payments, collection-lag schedules and cash budgets belong to BUD.
- Profit-based income tax calculation belongs to STM5. Accrual interest classification alone belongs to STM1.
- Ratios such as efficiency or gearing belong to RAT. Project cash flows for investment appraisal belong to INV.
- Bonus issues and reserve transfers create no cash receipt. Equity statement recording belongs to EQT7.
- Superseded financing products are historical only and are excluded (see OUT5). Physical notes and coins remain cash.

## How SCSA asks it
- Short-answer parts ask for a definition or example (CSH1), then a reconciliation of one or two accounts (CSH2–CSH4), often as the first part of a larger cash flow question.
- A typical extended question combines several reconciliations to build a partial cash flow statement, then asks for an interpretation (CSH12) or the benefits to a named user (CSH11).
- Stimulus data is given as account balances, with the working expected in the answer. Keys award marks for each balance used in the right place, so an unshown balance loses that mark.
- Frequency (notes file, papers 2020–2025): CSH5 interest appears on 5 of 6 papers; CSH1–CSH4, CSH7, CSH8 and CSH11 on 3–4 papers; CSH6, CSH9, CSH12 on 2 papers; CSH10 on 1 paper.

## CSH1: Explain cash and cash equivalents
- **Knowledge atoms**: CSH.K1 (cash), CSH.K2 (cash equivalents), CSH.K20 (cash-management overdrafts).
- **Recognise it**: "describe" or "explain" cash or cash equivalents under AASB 107, with an example of each. Multiple-choice versions differ by one condition, such as short-term against long-term, or insignificant against other risk of value change.
- **Method**: define cash (cash on hand and demand balances); define a cash equivalent with all three conditions (short-term, highly liquid and readily convertible to a known amount, insignificant risk of value change; the course definition normally uses a maturity of no more than three months); give one example of each that meets every condition, such as a short-term term deposit.
- **Marks**: one for a relevant fact about cash, one for a relevant fact about cash equivalents, one for a valid cash example, one for a valid cash-equivalent example. Multiple choice: the option stating all the conditions.
- **Traps**: a long-term investment given as a cash equivalent; a term deposit treated as qualifying without checking its term; leaving out the value-change condition. A cash-management overdraft repayable on demand may be included in the cash total (CSH.K20), but an overdraft is not automatically a cash equivalent.
- **Past questions**: 2025 SA Q18(b), 2024 EA Q23(e), 2021 MC Q14.

## CSH2: Reconcile cash received from customers
- **Knowledge atoms**: CSH.K3.
- **Recognise it**: operating activities extract; stimulus gives receivables balances, credit sales, sales returns and sometimes cash sales.
- **Method**: cash from credit customers = opening receivables + credit sales − sales returns (also discounts and write-offs as given) − closing receivables. Add cash sales if given. Total, check, and place in operating activities. Show each line.
- **Marks**: each balance used (opening receivables, credit sales, closing receivables), returns deducted, cash sales included, total stated.
- **Traps**: not deducting returns; adding the closing balance instead of subtracting it; leaving out cash sales.
- **Past questions**: 2023 SA Q20(a), 2021 SA Q21(a), 2020 SA Q20.

## CSH3: Reconcile cash paid to suppliers
- **Knowledge atoms**: CSH.K4, CSH.K5.
- **Recognise it**: cost of sales or cost of goods sold, opening and closing inventory, opening and closing accounts payable.
- **Method**: purchases = cost of sales + closing inventory − opening inventory. Cash paid to suppliers = opening payables + purchases − closing payables. Start from cost of sales, not sales revenue. Show purchases and payables workings separately.
- **Marks**: opening and closing inventory used in purchases; cost of sales used; opening and closing payables used; total cash paid.
- **Traps**: starting from sales revenue; reversing the inventory adjustment; subtracting opening payables. A purchase figure alone is not cash paid.
- **Past questions**: 2023 SA Q20(a), 2021 SA Q21(a), 2020 SA Q20.

## CSH4: Reconcile other operating cash payments
- **Knowledge atoms**: CSH.K6, CSH.K7.
- **Recognise it**: several operating expense lines with prepayments or accruals (prepaid insurance or rent, accrued wages, other accruals); request for cash paid rather than expense.
- **Method**: for each expense line:
  - Prepayment (asset): cash paid = expense + closing prepayment − opening prepayment.
  - Accrual (liability): cash paid = expense + opening accrual − closing accrual.
  Remove non-cash items such as depreciation from the expenses first. Total the payments and check each direction.
- **Marks**: opening and closing prepayments used; expense amounts used; opening and closing accruals used; other and administration expenses included; total operating cash payments.
- **Traps**: adding where the adjustment should subtract (or the reverse); leaving depreciation in; confusing prepayments (assets) with accruals (liabilities).
- **Past questions**: 2023 SA Q20(a), 2021 SA Q21(a), 2020 SA Q20.

## CSH5: Calculate interest received and paid
- **Knowledge atoms**: CSH.K8.
- **Recognise it**: interest income or expense with accrued interest receivable or payable; an investment balance and rate; a financing or operating section that includes interest.
- **Method**:
  - Interest received = interest income + opening accrued interest receivable − closing accrued interest receivable.
  - If only a balance and rate are given: interest = balance × rate × (time adjustment if not a full year), then use it as interest received.
  - Interest paid = interest expense + opening interest payable − closing interest payable.
  Place each total in the section the question asks for.
- **Marks**: balance and rate used; accrued interest income or payable used at both dates; interest income or expense; cash total.
- **Traps**: using interest expense as cash paid without the accrual adjustment; applying the rate to the wrong balance; adding the closing accrual instead of subtracting it.
- **Past questions**: 2025 SA Q18(a), 2023 SA Q20(a), 2022 SA Q19(a), 2021 SA Q21(a), 2020 SA Q20.

## CSH6: Determine cash income tax paid
- **Knowledge atoms**: CSH.K9.
- **Recognise it**: an "income tax paid" line in operating activities; the question asks for the cash tax payment for the year rather than the tax expense.
- **Method**: identify the cash tax paid reported for the period, as stated in the question or its working, and show it as an operating outflow. Do not use income tax expense as the cash figure. Where the stimulus gives tax payable balances only, a standard reconciliation is tax expense + opening tax payable − closing tax payable. This formula is not in the source notes; confirm it against the course booklet before use.
- **Marks**: the cash tax figure, shown as an operating outflow (2 marks in the bank).
- **Traps**: substituting tax expense; the accrual and cash values need not agree. Profit-based tax calculation belongs to STM5.
- **Past questions**: 2023 SA Q20(a), 2021 SA Q21(a).

## CSH7: Reconcile asset and investment purchase payments
- **Knowledge atoms**: CSH.K10.
- **Recognise it**: cost balances of equipment, machinery, land, vehicles, investments or intangibles; disposals at cost; revaluations mentioned; request for payments for assets in investing activities.
- **Method**: for each asset account, purchases = closing cost − opening cost + cost of assets disposed of. Exclude revaluations and other non-cash movements. For investments with no non-cash movement given, purchases = closing − opening balance. Show each asset separately, then total as an investing outflow.
- **Marks**: opening and closing cost used; disposals at cost added back; each asset's payment calculated (land, machinery, investments, intangibles); revaluation kept out.
- **Traps**: forgetting to add back disposals at cost; counting a revaluation as a cash payment; an increase in carrying amount is not automatically a cash purchase; missing an intangible category.
- **Past questions**: 2025 SA Q18(a), 2023 SA Q20(a), 2021 SA Q21(a), 2020 SA Q20.

## CSH8: Determine asset disposal cash proceeds
- **Knowledge atoms**: CSH.K11.
- **Recognise it**: an asset sold; stimulus gives cost, accumulated depreciation, depreciation expense, and a gain or loss on sale.
- **Method**:
  1. Accumulated depreciation removed = opening accumulated depreciation + depreciation expense − closing accumulated depreciation.
  2. Carrying amount sold = cost of asset sold − accumulated depreciation removed.
  3. Proceeds = carrying amount + gain, or carrying amount − loss.
  Show proceeds as an investing inflow.
- **Marks**: cost used; opening and closing accumulated depreciation used; depreciation expense included; carrying amount; gain or loss applied correctly; proceeds.
- **Traps**: adding a loss instead of subtracting it; using cost where carrying amount is needed; forgetting depreciation expense in the accumulated depreciation step. The gain or loss is not itself the cash receipt.
- **Past questions**: 2025 SA Q18(a), 2023 SA Q20(a), 2021 SA Q21(a), 2020 SA Q20.

## CSH9: Reconcile financing cash movements
- **Knowledge atoms**: CSH.K12, CSH.K13.
- **Recognise it**: financing section with share capital, loans or borrowings, retained earnings, reserve transfers, share issue costs and dividends.
- **Method**:
  - Share proceeds = closing share capital − opening share capital, excluding any bonus issue. Show share issue costs paid as a separate outflow.
  - Borrowings = closing loan − opening loan, with repayments shown separately where given.
  - Dividends paid = opening retained earnings + profit for the year − transfers to reserves − closing retained earnings.
  Place each line in financing and total.
- **Marks**: share movement; issue costs deducted; loan balances used; dividends from retained earnings including the reserve transfer; total financing flow.
- **Traps**: leaving profit out of the retained earnings reconciliation; forgetting the reserve transfer; treating issue costs as part of share proceeds; treating a bonus issue as a cash receipt.
- **Past questions**: 2022 SA Q19(a), 2020 SA Q20.

## CSH10: Reconcile opening and closing cash totals
- **Knowledge atoms**: CSH.K14.
- **Recognise it**: reconciliation of cash and cash equivalents at the start and end of the year, from statement of financial position figures for cash at bank, petty cash and term deposits.
- **Method**: at each date, list cash at bank, petty cash, and any term deposit that meets the CSH1 conditions. Total each date. Check that the change between the totals agrees with the net change in cash in the statement.
- **Marks**: opening and closing cash and petty cash; closing qualifying term deposit; the two totals.
- **Traps**: leaving out petty cash; using the bank balance only; including an investment that does not meet the cash-equivalent conditions.
- **Past questions**: 2020 SA Q20.

## CSH11: Explain the benefits of cash-flow information
- **Knowledge atoms**: CSH.K18, CSH.K19.
- **Recognise it**: identify or describe benefits of the statement of cash flows to internal or external users; multiple-choice items on what the statement shows about the business.
- **Method**: identify the user named (manager, lender, shareholder). Name one different benefit per mark, such as ability to meet debts, ability to generate cash, the link between profit and net cash flow, the effect of deferring receipts or payments, or assessment of operating cash flow. Explain how it helps that user's decision, linked to a stimulus figure or fact. Multiple choice: the option about liquidity, solvency or financial structure.
- **Marks**: each separate benefit identified and explained; benefit linked to a user. Where the key accepts more responses than marks, the stated mark count is the maximum.
- **Traps**: naming a benefit without explaining it; repeating one benefit in different words; no reference to the stimulus. Examiners report answers lacked depth and specificity.
- **Past questions**: 2023 MC Q14, 2022 EA Q22(e), 2021 SA Q21(b), 2020 MC Q9.

## CSH12: Interpret cash-flow categories and business implications
- **Knowledge atoms**: CSH.K15, CSH.K16, CSH.K17, CSH.K18.
- **Recognise it**: a statement with operating, investing and financing totals and opening and closing cash; request to describe the change in cash and analyse what drove it.
- **Method**:
  1. Describe the overall change from opening to closing cash, including any move from positive to negative.
  2. Classify each total as operating, investing or financing and state its sign.
  3. Compare the operating inflow with the payments it must cover, such as dividends.
  4. Analyse financing: loan repayments, dividends, share proceeds, and any resulting overdraft.
  5. State the implication of each point and cite the stimulus figures.
- **Marks**: overall change described; operating inflow analysed against dividends; financing flows analysed; for multiple choice, the interpretation matching the signs of net flows.
- **Traps**: describing figures without analysing them; misreading the sign of a net flow; treating categories in isolation. Do not conclude that the company generates enough cash from profit alone. Dividend classification follows the course booklet.
- **Past questions**: 2024 MC Q14, 2024 EA Q23(c), 2021 SA Q21(b).

## Traps (examiners' reports and keys, across CSH)
- **Workings not shown**: keys in CSH2–CSH9 award marks per balance used. Show every balance and line.
- **Reconciliation direction**: returns deducted; closing receivables subtracted; accrued expenses added back; opening payables added. Check the sign of every adjustment.
- **Theory thin**: CSH1 and CSH11 turn on full definitions and specific benefits. State them in full.
- **Stimulus unused**: CSH11 and CSH12 reward references to the stimulus figures.
- **Time**: extended answers lose marks when later parts are not attempted. Budget time across parts.

## Generator notes
- Build each question from one to three reconciliations (CSH2–CSH9), plus a theory part (CSH1, CSH10 or CSH11) and, for extended items, an interpretation (CSH12).
- Give balances at both dates for each account, and make the reconciliation work exactly (no rounding needed). Include a non-cash item (depreciation, revaluation or bonus issue) where the pattern requires exclusion.
- For CSH1 items, include one distractor that fails exactly one condition (for example a long-term deposit).
- For CSH12 items, make at least one net flow negative so the sign analysis is tested.
- Use the mark allocations in the table above as a guide only. Check the calibrated marks against the marking key before publishing a paper-style section.
- Do not use RAT12 (a cancelled 2021 administrative item) or any OUT pattern in generated current content.
- Verify: recompute every reconciliation from the stated balances; confirm the closing cash total agrees with the net change; check each sign against the direction rules above.
