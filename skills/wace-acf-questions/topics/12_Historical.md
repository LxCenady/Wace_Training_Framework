## Patterns

### OUT1 · Explain historical corporate social-disclosure requirements
- **Recognise it**: a stimulus about a company's social or environmental reporting, then a standalone question on who uses that disclosure and why, on the difficulties of preparing it, or on two disclosure examples. Verbs: "explain the use made of", "describe three difficulties", "explain the nature of ... through two examples".
- **Method**
  1. Find the company's reported social or environmental information in the stimulus (a reported result or figure).
  2. For a use or user question, name who uses it (the company itself, then other users) and say what each does with it. Tie each use to a specific reported result, not to CSR in general.
  3. For a difficulties question, give three different difficulties, each from a different area (for example resources, measurement standards, reporting standards, format and location, timing, incomplete or unbalanced disclosure). One per line.
  4. For a two-example question, give two distinct disclosure examples and explain each in full before moving on.
  5. Keep each point to one clear explanation sentence plus the link to the stimulus.
- **Key awards (per notes)**: for the use question, two awards, one for how the company uses its own CSR data and one for how users use CSR information, each referenced to a reported result. For the difficulties question the key lists six award categories, one per distinct difficulty: resources for measuring and reporting; no consistent measurement standards; no reporting standards, so presentation varies; differing locations and formats that hinder comparison; reporting timetables that do not match financial reporting dates; incomplete or unbalanced disclosure that omits unfavourable information. The question asks for three. The mark per difficulty is not given in the evidence pack. For the example question, a 3-mark explanation for each of two examples.
- **Traps**: stating what CSR is without naming a use or a user; describing one difficulty twice in different words; a general list of disclosures instead of two examples explained in depth; facts about the topic that are not linked to the stimulus.
- **Not**: current climate-risk, AASB S2 or sustainability-report interpretation belongs to GOV. A social context alone does not make an award historical.

### OUT2 · Evaluate historical responsible-practice costs and income
- **Recognise it**: "outline two costs" and "two potential sources of income" from socially or environmentally responsible practices, or a discuss version that weighs costs against potential income.
- **Method**
  1. Identify whether the practice is socially or environmentally responsible.
  2. Write two distinct costs first.
  3. Write two distinct income sources next, clearly separate from the costs.
  4. For a discuss version, weigh costs against potential income for the company, referring to the stimulus.
  5. Check that both sides are covered before moving on.
- **Key awards (per notes)**: outline version, 2 marks for two costs and 2 marks for two income sources (two distinct points on each side). Discuss version, a single award for discussing costs and potential income of environmentally responsible practices within a 4-mark part. The mark split inside that award is not given in the evidence pack.
- **Traps**: giving only one side; two points that are really the same point; discussing costs with no income, or the reverse.
- **Not**: ordinary cost calculations, or current climate-related opportunity analysis, are not OUT2 just because a responsible-business context appears.

### OUT3 · Explain historical annual-report and standalone KPI purposes
- **Recognise it**: a question on why annual reports are prepared and whom they inform, together with how managers use KPIs for accountability and for decision-making.
- **Method**
  1. State why annual reports are prepared and who they inform.
  2. Explain how managers use KPIs for accountability: actual results against targets.
  3. Explain how managers use KPIs for decision-making: making or revising decisions when results diverge from targets.
  4. Keep the accountability and decision-making explanations separate, so each can earn its own mark.
  5. Refer to the company's KPIs in the stimulus where it helps.
- **Key awards (per notes)**: three awards in order, (a) describing why annual reports are prepared or whom they inform, (b) explaining how managers use KPIs for accountability against targets, (c) explaining how managers use KPIs to make or revise decisions. The part is worth 8 marks. The evidence pack gives no mark per award.
- **Traps**: describing annual reports in general without naming who they inform; treating accountability and decision-making as one point; listing KPIs without explaining how managers use them.
- **Not**: interpreting an actual efficiency ratio against a benchmark stays RAT. Master-budget control stays BUD even when an old question calls it a KPI.

### OUT4 · Explain historical reporting institutions
- **Recognise it**: a 4-mark request to discuss the importance of a named reporting body or lobby group that is no longer on the current reporting-body list.
- **Method**
  1. Name the body and state its role in the financial-reporting framework.
  2. Explain its oversight of that framework and its standards-setting processes.
  3. Explain why that oversight matters to users of financial statements, in one or two sentences.
  4. Keep to the body's role, not general accounting theory.
- **Key awards (per notes)**: one 4-mark award for discussing the body's importance, covering its oversight of the reporting framework and its standards-setting processes.
- **Traps**: describing the body without saying why it matters; general statements about standards not linked to the named body.
- **Not**: ASIC, IASB, ISSB, AASB and ASX roles on the current list belong to GOV. The general benefits of standards belong to FRM6.

### OUT5 · Recognise superseded named financing products (no past question)
- **Recognise it**: a question that names a financing note or trust product specifically superseded, asked as historical practice.
- **Method**: check the question asks for historical practice; then identify the named superseded note or trust product.
- **Key awards**: none recorded in the evidence pack, so no award order can be stated.
- **Not**: underlying unsecured borrowing may remain FIN. Physical notes and coins are cash. Notes to financial statements remain STM.
- **Status**: examined false, no past question. Do not invent a frequency.

### OUT6 · Recognise historical recommended-dividend treatment (no past question)
- **Recognise it**: a question on a recommendation-only dividend requirement, asked explicitly as practice of superseded content.
- **Method**: identify the recommendation-only requirement named; answer on the historical treatment only, and only when the question explicitly asks for superseded content.
- **Key awards**: none recorded in the evidence pack.
- **Not**: declared or paid dividends remain EQT. A recommendation alone is not a current dividend declaration entry.
- **Status**: examined false, no past question. Do not invent a frequency.

## Where marks are lost (from the examiners' reports, as summarised in the notes)
- Answers state facts about the topic without tying them to the stimulus. Tie each explanation to a named company, result or figure.
- A key award that names a reported result earns nothing from a general statement. Cite the actual item.
- Students stop when one part is unanswered. Attempt the remaining parts; they can still earn marks.
- Two-sided parts lose half the marks when one side is missing.
