# CI · Sample proportions & confidence intervals 样本比例与置信区间 (Unit 4.3)

Bank: `<ROOT>\MAM\topics\07_Sample_Proportions_CI.md` / `.pdf` — 29 questions, 316 marks; almost always calculator-assumed (2 CF / 19 CA primary).

## Scope
- Random sampling and bias (convenience, voluntary response, timing, location) — explain whether a method is biased (2017 A12, 2023 A7, 2024 A10).
- Sample proportion p̂; approximate distribution p̂ ~ N(p, p(1 − p)/n) for large n; simulation-generated distributions (2016 A14, 2016S F9, 2023 A10).
- P(p̂ < c) with the normal approximation.
- CI: p̂ ± z√(p̂(1 − p̂)/n); margin of error; width; minimum n: ALWAYS use p̂ = 0.5 (worst case) even if a sample p̂ is known (2023 A7(c), 2024 A10(f), 2025 A16(e); 2023 report), unless the question says to use a given sample proportion (2016 A10(d)); 2018 A13 proves p̂ = 0.5 maximises E with calculus.
- Interpretation: meaning of "95% confidence", whether a claimed value is supported, effect of n and confidence level on width, how many of k intervals contain p (binomial, 2016 A20, 2024 A9).

## How SCSA asks it
- 7–21 mark CA questions: calculator survey (2016 A10), lullabees (2017 A18), mangoes (2018 A17), Big Foods (2019 A8), skipping breakfast (2019 A13), sample size + factors (2019 A14), business failures (2020 A12, 21 marks), library use sample size (2020 A14), new party vote (2021 A11), free-range eggs (2022 A13), coin flips (2024 A9), physical money claim (2024 A11), athletes' asthma (2025 A16).
- CF: margin halves ⇒ n × 4 (2017 F4); width/3 ⇒ n × 9 (2018 F5).

## Writing tips
- Always add an interpretation/decision part; keys reward explicitly checking whether the claimed value lies in the interval.
- z = 1.645 / 1.960 / 2.576; state rounding.
