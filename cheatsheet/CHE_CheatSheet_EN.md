# WACE Chemistry ATAR · Cheat Sheet (Patterns + Notes)

**General rules**: Data-booklet content (periodic table, E° table, constants, solubility rules, colours, amino acid structures, pH and ppm formulae) need not be rewritten, but know where it is · Explain with collision theory; Le Châtelier's principle only predicts · Equations: state symbols, species involved only, conditions (catalyst, acidic, heating) · Units on every value; round only at the end · No abbreviations such as NVR or NR; spell out any abbreviation at first use · No anthropomorphism (enzyme sensitive, molecules swarm, hydrophobic "hates" water) · No arrows for increase/decrease; use words.

## 1 Equilibrium and rate (EQ)

**① Dynamic equilibrium (EQ3)**: Closed system: forward and reverse rates equal, macroscopic properties constant; concentrations not necessarily equal (an open system loses substances, so equilibrium cannot form).

**② Le Châtelier's principle (EQ4)**
- Add reactant / remove product ⇒ right; add product / remove reactant ⇒ left; solids and pure liquids do not affect equilibrium.
- Increase pressure (decrease volume) ⇒ side with fewer gas molecules; equal gas moles ⇒ no shift.
- Inert gas: constant volume ⇒ no shift; constant pressure ⇒ side with more gas molecules. ⚠ State constant volume or pressure.
- Catalyst: no shift; forward and reverse rates rise equally; K unchanged.
- Increase temperature ⇒ endothermic direction. Only temperature changes K.

**③ Collision theory (EQ5)**: Adding reactant makes the forward rate jump; the reverse rate rises as product forms until they are equal again. Effective collision: energy ≥ Ea and correct orientation. ⚠ Explanations must use "effective collisions", not Le Châtelier.

**④ Equilibrium constant (EQ7–8)**: K = [products]^n / [reactants]^m; pure solids and liquids excluded. Reversing the equation ⇒ reciprocal K; multiplying coefficients by n ⇒ K^n. Large K ⇒ more products; only temperature changes K.

**⑤ Energy and rate graphs (EQ2, EQ6)**: ΔH = Ea(forward) − Ea(reverse); a catalyst lowers both Ea, ΔH unchanged. Concentration–time: no abrupt change at disturbance; rate–time: rate jumps.

**⑥ Colours (EQ9)**: CrO₄²⁻ (yellow) ⇌ Cr₂O₇²⁻ (orange): acid ⇒ deeper orange; base ⇒ yellow. Fe³⁺ (pale brown) + SCN⁻ ⇌ [Fe(SCN)]²⁺ (blood red). [Co(H₂O)₆]²⁺ (pink) ⇌ [CoCl₄]²⁻ (blue): concentrated HCl or heating ⇒ blue (forward endothermic); water ⇒ pink.

**⑦ Real-world contexts (EQ10)**: Ocean acidification: CO₂ + H₂O ⇌ H₂CO₃ ⇌ H⁺ + HCO₃⁻; H⁺ reacts with CO₃²⁻, shifting CaCO₃ formation left, so shells form less readily. Blood pH: hyperventilation (CO₂ falls) ⇒ left shift, pH rises. ⚠ Ocean pH is still above 7; shells are harder to form, not dissolved.

## 2 Acids and bases (AB)

**① Models (AB1–2)**: Arrhenius: acids give H⁺, bases give OH⁻ in water; cannot explain NH₃ as a base. Brønsted–Lowry: acid = proton donor, base = proton acceptor; conjugate pairs differ by one H⁺ (HCl/Cl⁻, NH₄⁺/NH₃). Amphiprotic species (H₂O, HCO₃⁻, HSO₄⁻) donate and accept H⁺. ⚠ Strong/weak = degree of ionisation; concentrated/dilute = concentration; ionisation (covalent → ions) ≠ dissociation (ions separate in an ionic crystal).

**② Ionisation and Ka (AB2)**: Ka = [H⁺][A⁻]/[HA]; larger Ka ⇒ stronger acid. Strong acids ionise almost completely, weak acids partially. Polyprotic acids ionise stepwise: H₂SO₄ first step complete, second incomplete (HSO₄⁻ ⇌ H⁺ + SO₄²⁻). Weak acid solution contains HA, H₂O, H⁺, A⁻, OH⁻; strong acid solution contains almost no HA.

**③ Salt hydrolysis (AB3)**: Strong acid–strong base salt (NaCl) ⇒ neutral; strong acid–weak base (NH₄Cl) ⇒ acidic; weak acid–strong base (CH₃COONa, Na₂CO₃) ⇒ basic. Amphiprotic salts: NaHCO₃ slightly basic; NaHSO₃ acidic. ⚠ Use relative [H₃O⁺] and [OH⁻]; do not just say "the anion hydrolyses".

**④ Buffers (AB4)**: Weak acid + conjugate base (CH₃COOH/CH₃COO⁻), or weak base + conjugate acid (NH₃/NH₄⁺). Strong acid–strong base, or strong acid with its salt, is not a buffer. Add H⁺: H⁺ + A⁻ → HA; add OH⁻: OH⁻ + HA → A⁻ + H₂O; the equilibrium shift cancels the change, so pH changes little. Greatest capacity at [HA] ≈ [A⁻], where pH ≈ pKa. ⚠ Dilution leaves pH almost unchanged but lowers buffer capacity.

**⑤ pH (AB5)**: pH = −log[H⁺]; at 25 °C pOH = 14 − pH (Kw = 1.00 × 10⁻¹⁴). Strong base: [OH⁻] = base concentration ⇒ [H⁺] = Kw / [OH⁻]. Decimal places of pH = significant figures of [H⁺].

**⑥ Titration and indicators (AB6–8)**: Equivalence point = exact reaction; end point = indicator change; they may differ slightly. Indicators (not in booklet, memorise): methyl orange red (pH < 3.1) → yellow (> 4.4); bromothymol blue yellow (< 6.0) → blue (> 7.6); phenolphthalein colourless (< 8.2) → pink (> 10.0). The indicator range must lie within the pH jump: strong acid–weak base (equivalence pH < 7) use methyl orange; weak acid–strong base (pH > 7) use phenolphthalein; strong–strong either. ⚠ Explain the choice using the ions at equivalence (e.g. CH₃COO⁻ hydrolysis gives basic). Rinse burette with its own solution, pipette with its own solution, conical flask with distilled water only (rinsing with analyte adds solute; result too high). Primary standard: high purity, stable in air, large molar mass, soluble, reacts completely in stoichiometric ratio; e.g. KHC₈H₄O₄ (KHP), anhydrous Na₂CO₃. NaOH and HCl are not primary standards.

**⑦ Non-redox reactions (AB9)**: Precipitates: Ag⁺ + Cl⁻ white (AgCl); Ag⁺ + Br⁻ or I⁻ pale yellow (AgBr, AgI); Ba²⁺ + SO₄²⁻ white (BaSO₄). Hydroxides: Cu(OH)₂ blue; Fe(OH)₂ green; Fe(OH)₃ reddish-brown; Mg(OH)₂, Al(OH)₃ white; Al(OH)₃ and Zn(OH)₂ dissolve in excess NaOH. Heating blue Cu(OH)₂ or green CuCO₃ turns both black (CuO).

## 3 Redox and electrochemistry (RX)

**① Oxidation numbers (RX1)**: Element 0; monatomic ion = charge; compound sums to 0. O usually −2 (peroxides −1); H usually +1 (metal hydrides −1); F always −1; Group IA +1, IIA +2. Oxidation: number rises (loses e⁻); reduction: number falls (gains e⁻); oxidising agent is reduced, reducing agent oxidised.

**② Balancing half-reactions (RX2)**: Acidic: balance atoms other than H and O → O with H₂O → H with H⁺ → charge with e⁻ → multiply so electrons match, then add. Basic: add OH⁻ to both sides to neutralise H⁺. Multiplying a half-reaction does not multiply E°.

**③ Standard electrode potentials (RX4)**: E° is intensive; the booklet states only 25 °C (textbook standard: 1.00 mol L⁻¹, 100 kPa). Larger E° ⇒ more easily reduced (stronger oxidant). E°cell = E°(reduction) − E°(oxidation), both written as reductions; E°cell > 0 ⇒ spontaneous. Measured voltage differs from E° due to non-standard concentration, temperature, internal resistance and overpotential.

**④ Galvanic and electrolytic cells (RX5–6)**: Mnemonic: An Ox (anode oxidation), Red Cat (cathode reduction). Galvanic (spontaneous): anode negative, cathode positive; electrons flow anode → external circuit → cathode, never through the electrolyte; salt bridge anions move to the anode, cations to the cathode. Electrolytic (external supply, non-spontaneous): anode to positive terminal, cathode to negative; oxidation still at anode. Notation: anode | anode ions || cathode ions | cathode (single line = phase boundary; double = salt bridge). Electroplating: object is the cathode, plating metal the anode. Copper refining: impure Cu anode, pure Cu cathode; Ag and Au settle as anode mud.

**⑤ Practical batteries (RX7)**: Alkaline zinc–manganese: primary (not rechargeable). Lead–acid: secondary; discharge consumes H₂SO₄, so density falls. Acidic H₂–O₂ fuel cell: negative 2H₂ → 4H⁺ + 4e⁻; positive O₂ + 4H⁺ + 4e⁻ → 2H₂O; only product is water, but H₂ storage, safety and cost are disadvantages.

**⑥ Corrosion and protection (RX8)**: Iron anode: Fe → Fe²⁺ + 2e⁻; cathode O₂ + 2H₂O + 4e⁻ → 4OH⁻; Fe²⁺ → Fe³⁺, forming rust Fe₂O₃·xH₂O. Needs water and oxygen; salt accelerates. Protection: barrier (paint, grease); sacrificial anode (Zn, Mg, more reactive than Fe; jetty piles, hulls); impressed current (iron to negative terminal). ⚠ Galvanising still protects after scratching (Zn is sacrificial); tin plating accelerates corrosion once scratched.

**⑦ Redox observations (RX3)**: Zn + CuSO₄: blue fades, salmon-red solid forms. Cl₂(aq) + KBr(aq): colourless turns orange. Complete combustion: blue flame, only CO₂ and H₂O; incomplete: yellow, smoky, CO or carbon.

## 4 Organic chemistry (ORG)

**① Naming (ORG1–2)**: Suffixes: -ene, -ol, -al (chain end), -one, -oic acid, ester = alkyl + alkanoate, -amine, -amide (chain end). Steps: longest chain with the functional group → lowest locant → alphabetical substituents. ⚠ pentanamide (not pentamide); 2,3-dimethylbut-2-ene (all locants); ester alcohol part first (ethyl ethanoate). Show all bonds and correct atoms (HC–R, not CH–R); condensed formulae must show –COOH clearly.

**② Isomerism (ORG3)**: Chain, positional, functional group (e.g. ethanol and dimethyl ether for C₂H₆O), cis–trans. Cis–trans needs two different groups on each alkene carbon; not all alkyl compounds have it.

**③ Intermolecular forces (ORG4)**: Strength: hydrogen bonding > dipole–dipole > dispersion; large molecules accumulate dispersion forces, which often dominate. Boiling point: larger molar mass ⇒ stronger dispersion ⇒ higher; branching ⇒ lower; –OH, –NH₂, –COOH form H-bonds ⇒ higher. Solubility: solute and water must form H-bonds or dipole interactions; long chains reduce solubility; NaCl in water is ion–dipole. ⚠ Do not write only "like dissolves like"; name the forces and compare their strengths.

**④ Addition, oxidation, esterification (ORG5–7)**: Alkene addition with Br₂, H₂, HX, H₂O (industrially H₃PO₄); bromine water orange → colourless. Acidified K₂Cr₂O₇ (orange → green, Cr³⁺) or acidified KMnO₄ (purple → colourless or pale pink, Mn²⁺): primary alcohol → aldehyde (distilled off as formed) → carboxylic acid (reflux, excess oxidant); secondary → ketone; tertiary: no reaction. Aldehyde → acid, also turns dichromate green. Acid + alcohol ⇌ ester + water (conc. H₂SO₄, heat, reversible); alkaline hydrolysis (saponification) is irreversible, giving a carboxylate. Acid + carbonate: CO₂ bubbles. Amines are basic (nitrogen lone pair) and form salts with acids.

**⑤ Identification (ORG6, 8, 9)**: Write reagent + observation for each substance + conclusion (bromine water: alkene decolourises; acidified dichromate: primary/secondary alcohol turns green, tertiary unchanged; Na₂CO₃: carboxylic acid fizzes).

## 5 Polymers, amino acids and proteins (PRO)

**① Addition polymers (PRO1)**: The double bond opens and the monomer carbons bond; repeat unit in brackets, n outside, extension bonds at both ends. ⚠ Do not add end groups. Monomer from polymer: remove brackets, restore the double bond.

**② Condensation polymers (PRO2)**: Two functional groups per monomer: diol + dicarboxylic acid ⇒ polyester (–COO–, e.g. PET); diamine + dicarboxylic acid ⇒ polyamide (–CO–NH–, e.g. nylon 6,6). Each bond eliminates a small molecule (H₂O; HCl with acyl chlorides). Monomers from polymer: break C–O (ester) adding OH to the carbonyl end and H to the oxygen end; break C–N (amide) similarly.

**③ Plastics (PRO3)**: Thermoplastic: linear or lightly branched, weaker forces, softens, recyclable. Thermosetting: covalent cross-links form a network; does not melt; hard and heat-resistant. More branching ⇒ lower density, softer (LDPE); linear, closely packed ⇒ higher density, harder (HDPE). Polyester ester bonds can be hydrolysed. ⚠ State which of chain length, branching, cross-linking or intermolecular forces is involved.

**④ Amino acids (PRO4)**: General formula H₂N–CH(R)–COOH; all except glycine are chiral. Neutral pH: zwitterion H₃N⁺–CH(R)–COO⁻; acidic: cation; basic: anion. Side chains (from booklet structures): non-polar, polar uncharged, acidic (Asp, Glu), basic (Lys, Arg, His).

**⑤ Peptides (PRO5)**: Peptide bond –CO–NH–: –COOH of one amino acid condenses with –NH₂ of another, losing H₂O. Dipeptide: N-terminus (free NH₂) on the left, C-terminus (free COOH) on the right; read N → C (Gly–Ala ≠ Ala–Gly).

**⑥ Protein structure (PRO6)**: Primary: sequence joined by covalent peptide bonds. Secondary: α-helix and β-pleated sheet, held by H-bonds between backbone C=O and N–H. Tertiary: side-chain interactions: H-bonds; ionic bonds (–NH₃⁺ and –COO⁻); disulfide bonds (–S–S– between cysteines); dispersion forces (non-polar side chains). Denaturation (heat, strong acid or base, heavy metal ions, organic solvents) breaks H-bonds, ionic and disulfide bonds; peptide bonds remain intact. Mutation changes the primary sequence and may alter folding and function. ⚠ Do not say "heating breaks peptide bonds" or that hydrophobic groups "hate water"; say dispersion forces between non-polar side chains.

**⑦ Enzymes (PRO7)**: Protein biocatalysts; active site complementary in shape to the substrate, highly specific; optimum temperature and pH. Industrial: mild conditions, high selectivity; limits: denature at high temperature or extreme pH, costly, hard to recover. ⚠ pH changes the shape and charge of the active site and denatures the enzyme; do not write "enzyme is sensitive to pH".

## 6 Synthesis and industry (SYN)

**① Routes (SYN1)**: Alkene → alcohol (hydration); alcohol → aldehyde → acid (oxidation); acid + alcohol → ester. State reagents, conditions, intermediates and reaction type at each step.

**② Haber process (SYN2)**: N₂(g) + 3H₂(g) ⇌ 2NH₃(g), exothermic; ~450 °C, ~20 000 kPa, iron catalyst; unreacted gases recycled; NH₃ cooled, liquefied and separated. Compromise: low T ⇒ higher yield, slower rate; high T ⇒ faster, lower yield; high P raises both rate and yield, but adds equipment and energy cost; catalyst only raises rate.

**③ Contact process (SYN2)**: 2SO₂(g) + O₂(g) ⇌ 2SO₃(g), exothermic; V₂O₅ catalyst, ~450 °C, 100–200 kPa. SO₃ is absorbed in concentrated H₂SO₄ (water gives acid mist).

**④ Ethanol production (SYN1, 3)**: Hydration: C₂H₄(g) + H₂O(g) ⇌ C₂H₅OH(g), H₃PO₄ catalyst, ~300 °C, ~6 000–7 000 kPa; petroleum feedstock (non-renewable), fast, continuous, high purity, high energy use. Fermentation: C₆H₁₂O₆(aq) → 2C₂H₅OH(aq) + 2CO₂(g), yeast enzymes, 25–37 °C, atmospheric pressure, anaerobic, mostly batch; renewable, low energy, but slow and product only ~12–15%, needing distillation. Compare routes on renewability, energy, by-products and atom economy.

**⑤ Soaps and detergents (SYN4)**: Saponification: triglyceride + 3NaOH → glycerol + 3 fatty acid sodium salts (soap). Soap: hydrophilic head (–COO⁻ Na⁺) + hydrophobic tail; in a micelle the tails face inwards trapping oil and the heads face outwards. Hard water: Ca²⁺ and Mg²⁺ form insoluble salts (scum) with soap anions; calcium and magnesium salts of anionic detergents are soluble, so cleaning is unaffected.

**⑥ Biodiesel (SYN5)**: Transesterification: triglyceride + 3CH₃OH ⇌ glycerol + 3 fatty acid methyl esters. NaOH/KOH is fast, cheap and high-yielding but corrosive and forms soap; lipase is mild, forms no soap and can be reused (2020 key).

## 7 Stoichiometry and quantitative analysis (QNT)

**① Conversions and dilution (QNT1)**: n = m/M; n = cV; dilution c₁V₁ = c₂V₂ (not in booklet, memorise). g L⁻¹ ÷ molar mass ⇒ mol L⁻¹; ppm (mg/kg) ≈ mg L⁻¹ for dilute aqueous solutions. ⚠ Write volume, concentration, mass; do not use "amount" as a substitute.

**② Gases (QNT6)**: STP (0.0 °C, 100 kPa): molar volume 22.71 L mol⁻¹ (booklet value, not 22.4 L). PV = nRT: T in K (+273.15), P in kPa, V in L; R = 8.314 used with kPa·L.

**③ Limiting reagent and yield (QNT2–3)**: Limiting reagent = smallest n ÷ coefficient; theoretical yield is calculated from it. Yield = actual ÷ theoretical × 100%; purity = mass of pure substance ÷ mass of sample × 100%.

**④ Formulae (QNT5)**: For each element n = m/M; divide by the smallest to get a whole-number ratio; if not near an integer, multiply by 2, 3, etc. Combustion: n(C) = n(CO₂), n(H) = 2n(H₂O), O by difference. Molecular formula = (molar mass ÷ empirical formula mass) × empirical formula.

**⑤ Titration (QNT4)**: n(unknown) = n(titrant) × coefficient ratio; each mole of H₂SO₄ gives 2 mol H⁺. Scaling: 25.00 mL taken from 250 mL ⇒ multiply by 10. Average concordant titres (difference ≤ 0.10 mL), discarding rough titres. Back titration: add excess reagent first, then titrate the remainder.

**⑥ Precipitation gravimetry (QNT8)**: Precipitate completely → filter → wash with distilled water → dry to constant mass → weigh and calculate. ⚠ Incomplete drying makes the mass too high; insufficient washing makes the result too high.

**⑦ Significant figures (QNT7)**: Multiplication/division: fewest significant figures; addition/subtraction: fewest decimal places. Round only at the end, with units; exact numbers are not counted.

## 8 Science inquiry (INQ)

**① Design (INQ1–3)**: Hypothesis: testable, "If … then … because …". State independent, dependent and controlled variables and how they are controlled; include a control; repeat and average. Safety: state hazards, risks and protective measures.

**② Graphing and reading (INQ4–5)**: Label axes with quantity and unit, even scales; title "dependent variable against independent variable". Draw a smooth line of best fit with a ruler, not point-to-point. Show read-off lines on the graph; give readings to the correct decimal places (e.g. 4.8, not 5).

**③ Errors (INQ6)**: Random error: fluctuating readings, estimation; reduced by repeats and averaging. Systematic error: fixed-direction bias (e.g. burette not rinsed, instrument not calibrated, impure standard); repetition does not remove it. State whether the error makes the result higher or lower, and why. Reading uncertainty: half the smallest division (50 mL burette ±0.05 mL).

**④ Conclusions and observations (INQ7)**: Conclusions must be supported by data; state limitations (small sample, large error); do not write "proves" without strong evidence. ⚠ Solutions: "colourless" or the specific colour, not "clear"; solids: colour and form, not chemical name; metals: "silver-grey solid"; copper: "salmon-red solid". Gases: "bubbles", not "colourless odourless gas" (an inference). Record all observations before, during and after the reaction, including excess or limiting reagent.

**⑤ Gas tests (not in booklet)**: H₂: explosive pop; O₂: relights a glowing splint; CO₂: turns limewater milky; Cl₂: moist blue litmus turns red, then bleaches; NH₃: moist red litmus turns blue.
